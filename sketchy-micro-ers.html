1. Herpes Simplex Virus Types 1 & 2 (Herpesviridae)

Exam snapshot
  • Painful grouped vesicles on an erythematous base (cold sore, genital ulcers), recurring in the same dermatome
  • HSV-1: gingivostomatitis, herpes labialis, keratitis, temporal lobe encephalitis; HSV-2: genital herpes, neonatal herpes, aseptic meningitis
  • Diagnosis: PCR (CSF PCR for encephalitis/meningitis) or viral culture; Tzanck smear shows multinucleated giant cells but is nonspecific
  • Treatment: acyclovir/valacyclovir (needs viral thymidine kinase); foscarnet for acyclovir-resistant strains
  • Buzzwords: Cowdry A bodies, dendritic corneal ulcer, temporal lobe hemorrhagic necrosis, latency in trigeminal (HSV-1) and sacral (HSV-2) ganglia
  1. 1Hermes sprinting: HSV-1 and HSV-2 are DNA viruses of the Herpesviridae family. They are highly contagious and spread mainly by direct contact with infected skin, mucosa, or body fluids (including saliva and genital secretions), and also vertically from mother to baby (neonatal herpes, usually at delivery).
  2. 2Cool colors: HSV-1 and HSV-2 are DNA viruses (the cool colors group DNA viruses). Most DNA viruses replicate in the nucleus, which is why HSV produces intranuclear inclusions.
  3. 3Enveloping toga: HSV-1 and HSV-2 are enveloped viruses. The envelope (acquired from the host nuclear membrane) makes them fragile outside the body and explains why transmission needs close contact.
  4. 4Double road lines: HSV-1 and HSV-2 are double-stranded, linear DNA viruses.
  5. 5Cow spots: Intranuclear eosinophilic Cowdry type A inclusions are characteristic of cells infected with HSV (also seen with VZV and CMV). Infected cells (keratinocytes or neurons) show a clear nuclear halo, multinucleation, nuclear molding, and chromatin margination.
  6. 6Torch: HSV is part of the TORCHeS group of infections transmitted vertically across the placenta: T = Toxoplasma; O = Other (varicella-zoster virus, parvovirus B19, Zika virus, HIV); R = Rubella; C = CMV; H = HSV; S = Syphilis. Neonatal HSV, usually acquired at delivery from maternal genital infection, causes skin/eye/mouth lesions, encephalitis, or disseminated disease.
  7. 7Red lips: Primary HSV-1 infection often presents as gingivostomatitis, with painful vesicles and ulcerations inside the mouth and around the lips. It is most common in children.
  8. 8Lip ulcers: Herpes labialis (cold sores) presents as clusters of small, painful vesicles on a red base at the vermilion border of the lip. They rupture into shallow ulcers and heal with a yellow crust. It is most commonly caused by HSV-1.
  9. 9Serpent caduceus: Herpes keratoconjunctivitis, mainly HSV-1, causes eye pain, photophobia, redness, tearing, and decreased visual acuity. Fluorescein slit-lamp exam shows branching (dendritic, 'serpiginous') corneal ulcers, which are diagnostic. Treat with topical antivirals; topical steroids alone worsen it.⭐ Dendritic corneal ulcer on fluorescein = HSV keratitis.
  10. 10Red wings on helmet near temples: HSV-1 encephalitis is the most common cause of sporadic encephalitis in the U.S. It causes hemorrhagic necrosis of the temporal lobes, producing fever, seizures, altered mental status, personality changes, and olfactory hallucinations from limbic involvement. Diagnose with CSF PCR (MRI shows temporal lobe changes) and treat empirically with IV acyclovir.⭐ HSV encephalitis = temporal lobe + hemorrhage; start acyclovir before results.
  11. 11Black helmet: HSV-1 encephalitis is the most common cause of sporadic encephalitis in the U.S. It causes hemorrhagic necrosis of the temporal lobes, producing fever, seizures, altered mental status, personality changes, and olfactory hallucinations from limbic involvement. Diagnose with CSF PCR (MRI shows temporal lobe changes) and treat empirically with IV acyclovir.check original
  12. 12Crazy eyes: HSV-1 encephalitis is the most common cause of sporadic encephalitis in the U.S. It causes hemorrhagic necrosis of the temporal lobes, producing fever, seizures, altered mental status, personality changes, and olfactory hallucinations from limbic involvement. The altered mental status and behavioral change reflect temporal lobe/limbic damage. Diagnose with CSF PCR (MRI shows temporal lobe changes) and treat empirically with IV acyclovir.
  13. 13Three helmet gems: After primary infection HSV-1 remains latent in the trigeminal ganglia (sensory neurons). Reactivation by stress, sunlight (UV), fever, or immunosuppression causes cold sores, keratoconjunctivitis, or, rarely, temporal lobe encephalitis.
  14. 14Dew drops on roses: HSV lesions are painful grouped vesicles or pustules on an erythematous base, classically likened to 'dew drops on a rose petal.' They rupture into shallow ulcers, crust over as they heal, and recur because of viral latency.
  15. 15Dew drops on finger: Herpetic whitlow is an HSV infection of the finger with painful, clustered vesicles on an erythematous base. It is classically seen in healthcare workers and dentists (contact with oral secretions) and in thumb-sucking children.
  16. 16Target stamps on hand and arm: Erythema multiforme is a hypersensitivity reaction triggered by certain drugs and infections, with HSV being a classic infectious trigger. It produces target lesions (dusky center, pale edematous zone, outer erythematous ring) that start on the backs of the hands and feet and spread centrally.⭐ HSV is the most common infectious cause of erythema multiforme.
  17. 17Ruffles by groin: Initial genital herpes, often HSV-2 (HSV-1 is increasingly common), causes painful grouped vesicles on an erythematous base in the genital or perianal region, with shallow ulcers, dysuria, and tender inguinal lymphadenopathy. It is spread by sexual contact, including by asymptomatic shedding.
  18. 18Codpiece: HSV-2 establishes latency mainly in the sacral ganglia. Reactivation causes recurrent genital or perianal lesions, usually milder and shorter than the primary episode.
  19. 19Neck brace: HSV-2 is associated with aseptic (lymphocytic) meningitis, particularly during primary genital infection. Patients have headache, fever, and neck stiffness, with lymphocytic pleocytosis on CSF analysis (diagnose with CSF PCR). It can recur (Mollaret meningitis).
  20. 20Tank + giant spots: A Tzanck smear (scraping of a lesion base) shows multinucleated giant cells in HSV infection. It is nonspecific, since VZV also produces them, and has been replaced by PCR or viral culture for specific diagnosis.
  21. 21Violet recycling bin: Acyclovir and valacyclovir are used for treatment and for prophylaxis (suppressive therapy) against recurrent outbreaks. They are guanosine analogs activated by viral thymidine kinase and terminate viral DNA synthesis by inhibiting viral DNA polymerase. Resistance (thymidine kinase mutation) is treated with foscarnet.

2. Human Papillomavirus (Papillomaviridae)

Exam snapshot
  • Warts: common warts (HPV 1-4) in kids, anogenital warts (HPV 6, 11); cervical dysplasia/cancer (HPV 16, 18) with postcoital bleeding
  • Naked (non-enveloped) icosahedral dsDNA virus; E6 degrades p53, E7 inactivates Rb
  • Dx: Pap smear/HPV DNA testing; koilocytes (perinuclear halo, raisinoid nucleus); acetowhitening on colposcopy
  • Prevention: Gardasil 9 (recombinant L1 capsid vaccine, types 6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58); wart treatment is topical/destructive (podophyllotoxin, imiquimod, cryotherapy)
  • Buzzwords: koilocytes, cauliflower warts, E6 = p53, E7 = Rb, most common STI
  1. 1Pill bugs: Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a DNA virus in the family Papillomaviridae.
  2. 2Cool colors: Papillomaviruses are DNA viruses (circular double-stranded DNA).
  3. 3Statue of David: Papillomaviruses lack an envelope, so they are naked viruses.⭐ Naked viruses resist drying and detergents, so they spread well on surfaces.
  4. 4Children holding up 1 & 4 fingers: Common warts (verruca vulgaris) are caused by HPV types 1-4.
  5. 5Pill bugs on young kids: Common warts commonly affect children and young adults: firm, rough, hyperkeratotic papules with a verrucous surface, often on the hands and fingers; black dots from thrombosed capillaries are often seen on the surface.⭐ Pinpoint black dots in a wart are thrombosed capillaries.
  6. 6Kids playing together: Common warts spread by direct skin-to-skin contact or contaminated surfaces (fomites), especially through skin trauma or microabrasions.
  7. 7"11" and "6" kids: HPV types 6 and 11 cause anogenital warts (condyloma acuminata) and laryngeal papillomatosis.⭐ Low-risk types: 6 and 11 cause benign warts, not cancer.
  8. 8Eating bug: HPV 6 and 11 cause laryngeal (recurrent respiratory) papillomatosis: multiple exophytic, wart-like, whitish cauliflower-like papillomas on the vocal cords and larynx that can obstruct the airway and cause hoarseness, chronic cough or respiratory distress.⭐ Acquired by passage through an infected birth canal; presents in a child with hoarseness or stridor.
  9. 9Putting bug down pants: Anogenital warts (condyloma acuminata) are sexually transmitted and most often caused by HPV 6 and 11; they are soft, flesh-colored or pink, cauliflower-like papules or plaques on the genital and perianal areas.
  10. 10Accumulating bugs in jar: Anogenital warts (condyloma acuminata) are sexually transmitted and most often caused by HPV 6 and 11; lesions can multiply and coalesce into larger plaques in the genital and perianal areas.⭐ Treat with podophyllotoxin, imiquimod, trichloroacetic acid or cryotherapy; they are not a sign of cancer risk.
  11. 1116 year old's car keys: HPV types 16, 18, 31 and 33 are high-risk types linked to anogenital and squamous cell cancers, including cancers of the cervix, vulva, vagina, penis and anus.⭐ Types 16 and 18 cause about 70% of cervical cancers.
  12. 1218 year old's voting sticker: HPV types 16, 18, 31 and 33 are high-risk types linked to anogenital and squamous cell cancers (cervix, vulva, vagina, penis, anus).
  13. 13Crab by 16 and 18 y/o: HPV types 16, 18, 31 and 33 are high-risk types linked to anogenital and squamous cell cancers; HPV is also implicated in oropharyngeal cancers.
  14. 14Bug on seat: HPV types 16, 18, 31 and 33 are high-risk types linked to anogenital and squamous cell cancers, including cervix, vulva, vagina, penis and anus.
  15. 15Kissing couple: HPV is transmitted mainly by sexual contact (vaginal, anal or oral) and is the most common sexually transmitted infection worldwide.
  16. 16Syringe fence spikes: The original Gardasil vaccine is a recombinant subunit (L1 capsid protein) vaccine against HPV 6, 11, 16 and 18; it is no longer distributed in the U.S. because Gardasil 9 replaced it, protecting against nine types (6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58).⭐ Vaccine is non-infectious (virus-like particles); routinely given at age 11-12, before sexual exposure.
  17. 17Fence around 6, 11, 16, & 18 year olds: Gardasil protected against HPV 6, 11, 16 and 18 and has been replaced by Gardasil 9, which covers nine serotypes (6, 11, 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58).
  18. 18Children outside fence with 1 & 4 fingers: The HPV vaccines (Gardasil/Gardasil 9) do not cover HPV types 1-4, which cause common warts.
  19. 19"E" fork + "6" shrimp: The E6 protein of high-risk HPV (especially types 16 and 18) targets the p53 tumor suppressor for degradation, leading to uncontrolled cell proliferation and cancer risk.⭐ E6 = p53 degradation (via ubiquitin ligase E6AP).
  20. 20Crab cracker + cell cycle table: p53 is a regulatory checkpoint that halts the cell cycle at the G1-to-S transition when cellular stress or DNA damage is detected.
  21. 21"E" fork + "7" straw in root beer: The E7 protein of high-risk HPV inactivates the Rb tumor suppressor (releasing E2F), causing unchecked cell cycle progression and increasing cancer risk.⭐ E7 = Rb (7 and "root beer").
  22. 22Face smear: Pap smears screen for early cervical changes (dysplasia) that can progress to HPV-caused cervical cancer; postcoital bleeding can be an early manifestation of cervical dysplasia or carcinoma.⭐ Screening: cytology from age 21 (HPV co-testing from 30 per guidelines).
  23. 23Blue sunny-side up egg: Koilocytes, seen in HPV-infected cells of the cervical transformation zone, are squamous cells with perinuclear clearing (halo), enlarged irregular nuclei and frequent binucleation, resembling a sunny-side-up egg (halo = white, dense nucleus = yolk).⭐ Koilocytes are pathognomonic for HPV cytopathic effect.
  24. 24AIDS ribbon: HIV/AIDS and other immunosuppression (organ transplant, long-term corticosteroids) increase the risk of persistent HPV infection and complications such as anogenital warts, cervical dysplasia and cervical cancer, because of impaired viral clearance.⭐ Cervical cancer is AIDS-defining in HIV.
  25. 25Immunocompromised cane: Immunosuppressed patients (HIV/AIDS, transplant recipients, chronic corticosteroids) have impaired viral clearance, increasing risk of persistent HPV, warts, dysplasia and cervical cancer.

3. Candida albicans

Exam snapshot
  • Clinical clue: white scrapable thrush, curd-like vaginal discharge with itching, diaper rash with satellite lesions, esophagitis (CD4 <200), or candidemia in neutropenic or IV-catheter patients.
  • Microbiology: opportunistic yeast with pseudohyphae and budding; forms germ tubes at 37 C; normal flora of mouth, gut, and vagina; catalase positive.
  • Diagnosis: KOH prep showing budding yeast with pseudohyphae; germ tube test; culture; vaginal pH remains normal (4-4.5).
  • Treatment: nystatin or clotrimazole (topical/oral), fluconazole for most infections; echinocandins for severe/invasive disease; amphotericin B for refractory cases.
  • Buzzwords: 'germ tube', 'pseudohyphae', 'thrush', 'cottage cheese', 'satellite lesions', 'esophagitis AIDS-defining', 'CGD', 'neutropenia'.
  1. 1Canada: 'Can-dida': Candida albicans, an opportunistic fungus that causes both cutaneous and systemic infections.
  2. 2Dimorphic butterfly: C. albicans is dimorphic but differs from the classic dimorphic fungi: it grows as yeast at 23-25 C and forms filamentous (pseudohyphal/hyphal) growth at 37 C, the reverse of the usual temperature pattern.⭐ Classic dimorphs are mold at 25 C and yeast at 37 C.
  3. 3'37 flavors' + germ tube straw: C. albicans forms germ tubes (the start of hyphal growth) when incubated in serum at 37 C.⭐ Germ tube test is positive for C. albicans.
  4. 4'20 C' + yeast snowballs: At 23-25 C, C. albicans exists as yeast that reproduces by budding.
  5. 5Catalase cat: C. albicans is catalase positive (converts hydrogen peroxide to water and oxygen), so patients with chronic granulomatous disease are susceptible to infection.⭐ Catalase-positive organisms in CGD: S. aureus, Candida, Aspergillus, Nocardia, Serratia, Burkholderia.
  6. 6Open mouth + tube slide: Candida species are normal flora of the GI tract and oral cavity and can commonly contaminate sputum cultures, so isolation from sputum often reflects colonization.
  7. 7Baby in red swing: C. albicans commonly causes candidal diaper dermatitis, thriving in warm, moist skin folds, with erythematous, beefy-red, confluent plaques and satellite pustules or papules around the main rash.
  8. 8Immunocompromised cane: Immunocompromised patients, particularly those with neutropenia, are at higher risk of disseminated candidiasis.⭐ Neutropenia = disseminated candidiasis; T-cell defects = mucocutaneous disease.
  9. 9Snow on tongue + inhaler: C. albicans causes oral candidiasis (thrush) with white, scrapable plaques on the oral mucosa (leukoplakia does not scrape off). It is common with inhaled corticosteroids and immunocompromise.⭐ Rinse mouth after inhaled steroids.
  10. 10Shoveling snow: Oral thrush plaques can be scraped off (shoveled), leaving an erythematous base; this distinguishes thrush from non-scrapable leukoplakia, including hairy leukoplakia.
  11. 11KOH salt: KOH mount of scrapings from oral, vaginal, or skin lesions shows budding yeast and pseudohyphae.⭐ KOH dissolves human cells, leaving fungal elements.
  12. 12Snow inside tube slide: C. albicans can cause esophageal candidiasis in immunocompromised patients, especially with HIV/AIDS, with gray-white pseudomembranes, odynophagia, and dysphagia.⭐ Linear white plaques on endoscopy.
  13. 13'Max 200 LBS': Esophageal candidiasis is an AIDS-defining illness, most often seen when the CD4+ count is below 200 cells/mm3.
  14. 14Snowball thrown in crotch: C. albicans causes vulvovaginal candidiasis with intense pruritus and thick, white, curd-like ('cottage cheese') vaginal discharge.
  15. 15Candy: Vulvovaginal candidiasis can be precipitated by uncontrolled diabetes, antibiotic use, and hormonal changes (e.g., oral contraceptives). Hyperglycemia increases glucose in tissues and mucosal secretions, feeding Candida overgrowth.
  16. 16Medication bottles: Vulvovaginal candidiasis can be precipitated by uncontrolled diabetes, antibiotic use, and hormonal changes (e.g., oral contraceptives). Antibiotics kill protective vaginal flora (lactobacilli), allowing Candida to overgrow.
  17. 17Birth control pills: Vulvovaginal candidiasis can be precipitated by uncontrolled diabetes, antibiotic use, and hormonal changes (e.g., oral contraceptives). Estrogen increases glycogen in vaginal epithelial cells, which Candida uses as a nutrient.
  18. 18'Playground open until 4 pH': In vulvovaginal candidiasis the vaginal pH stays normal (about 3.8-4.5) because Candida tolerates acid and does not raise pH.⭐ Contrast with bacterial vaginosis and trichomoniasis (pH >4.5).
  19. 193 heart roofs: C. albicans can cause endocarditis, mostly of the tricuspid valve in IV drug users (contaminated venous blood reaches the right heart first), and also in patients with prosthetic valves or indwelling catheters.
  20. 20Pinecone: Azoles, especially fluconazole, are the antifungals of choice for a broad range of Candida albicans infections.⭐ Fluconazole is first-line for oropharyngeal, esophageal, and vaginal candidiasis.
  21. 21Amphibians: Amphotericin B is used for severe or fluconazole-resistant Candida infections (amphibian = ampho).
  22. 22'Play NYCE': Nystatin is effective for cutaneous and oral Candida infections (swish and swallow for thrush); it is too toxic for systemic use.
  23. 23Cap: Echinocandins (caspofungin, micafungin) are options for severe or refractory candidiasis, including esophageal and systemic disease; 'cap' = caspofungin.⭐ Echinocandins are first-line for candidemia.

4. Trichomonas vaginalis

Exam snapshot
  • Clue: sexually active woman with foul-smelling, frothy yellow-green discharge, vulvovaginal itching and dysuria; strawberry cervix.
  • Flagellated protozoan with an undulating membrane; trophozoite only (no cyst); infects squamous epithelium.
  • Diagnosis: wet mount showing motile pear-shaped trophozoites with jerky motility; vaginal pH above 4.5; NAAT.
  • Treatment: metronidazole or tinidazole for patient and all sexual partners.
  • Buzzwords: strawberry cervix, frothy discharge, tumbling motility, pH greater than 4.5, no cyst.
  1. 1Magic trichs: Trichomonas vaginalis is a unicellular protozoan parasite.
  2. 2Magic scarves: It is a motile organism with four anterior flagella and an undulating membrane.
  3. 3Trophy: T. vaginalis exists only as a trophozoite; it has no cyst form (so it survives poorly outside the host).⭐ No cyst form means transmission is almost exclusively sexual.
  4. 4Couple kissing: Trichomonas vaginalis is a sexually transmitted infection of the urogenital tract.
  5. 5Cobblestone sidewalk: T. vaginalis has a tropism for squamous epithelium and causes infection and inflammation of the vulva, vagina, cervix, and urethra.
  6. 6Smiling delivery man: Asymptomatic carriage, primarily in men, contributes to spread; treating sexual partners reduces this risk.
  7. 7Vaginal flowers: T. vaginalis causes vulvovaginitis: inflammation of the vulva and vagina with erythema, burning, itching, and discharge.
  8. 8Burning trash can: Burning sensation (dysuria and vulvovaginal irritation) is a feature of the vulvovaginitis.
  9. 9Itching dog: Pruritus is a feature of the vulvovaginitis.
  10. 10Leaking fluid: Vaginal discharge is a feature of the vulvovaginitis.
  11. 11Frothy green puddles: Trichomonas causes a classically copious, yellow-green, frothy, malodorous vaginal discharge.⭐ Frothy, malodorous green discharge is the classic descriptor.
  12. 12Strawberry hat trick: Cervical inflammation with erythema, edema, and punctate hemorrhages gives the 'strawberry cervix' (colpitis macularis).⭐ Strawberry cervix is specific but seen in a minority of cases.
  13. 13$4.50 and up: Trichomonas raises vaginal pH (basic environment), so pH testing shows 4.5 or greater.⭐ pH above 4.5 is also seen in bacterial vaginosis; BV has clue cells and a positive whiff test.
  14. 14Leaking package: In men, infection is usually asymptomatic but may cause urethritis with dysuria and a thin white discharge.
  15. 15Pear puddle: Saline wet mount shows motile, pear-shaped trophozoites with a characteristic jerky, tumbling motility.⭐ Immediate wet mount is the fastest diagnostic test; NAAT is more sensitive.
  16. 16Metro: Metronidazole is effective against Trichomonas vaginalis.⭐ Warn about the disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol.
  17. 17Metro couple: All sexual partners must be treated to prevent reinfection and further transmission; therapy of choice is metronidazole (or tinidazole).
  18. 18Condom hat: Consistent barrier contraception (condoms) prevents re-transmission until treatment is complete.

5. Gardnerella vaginalis

Exam snapshot
  • Bacterial vaginosis: thin gray-white homogeneous discharge with fishy odor, little or no itching, burning, or inflammation.
  • Gram-variable (gram-indeterminate) coccobacillus with a thin cell wall; overgrowth after loss of protective Lactobacillus.
  • Vaginal pH >4.5, positive whiff test with KOH, and clue cells on wet mount (Amsel criteria).
  • Metronidazole or clindamycin (oral or topical); not usually treating partners.
  • Clue cells, fishy odor, whiff test, pH >4.5, no inflammation (not an STI per se).
  1. 1"Fish garden": Gardnerella vaginalis is a gram-indeterminate (gram-variable) coccobacillus that causes bacterial vaginosis (BV).
  2. 2Purple and pink graffiti: Gardnerella has a thin gram-positive-type cell wall, but it stains inconsistently, appearing gram-positive or gram-negative, so it is called gram-variable.
  3. 3Overgrowth of Venus flytraps: Loss of the normal acid-producing, facultative anaerobic Lactobacillus flora raises vaginal pH and allows Gardnerella overgrowth; triggers include antibiotics, hormonal changes, sexual activity, and douching.⭐ BV is a polymicrobial overgrowth, not a classic STI.
  4. 4Venus flytrap discharge: BV causes a thin, gray-white vaginal discharge with a characteristic "fishy" odor; patients may be asymptomatic or have mild irritation, but itching, burning, and inflammation are typically absent.
  5. 5Fish market: Same presentation: thin gray-white discharge and "fishy" odor, particularly after intercourse or menses, with little inflammation.
  6. 6"p 4.5 and up": Vaginal pH in BV is above 4.5 (often 5.0-6.5), compared with a normal pH of 3.8-4.5.⭐ pH >4.5 is also seen in trichomoniasis.
  7. 7Sniffing dog + "KOH" hat: The whiff test: adding 10% KOH to the discharge produces a strong fishy odor (amines) in BV.⭐ Positive whiff test = BV (also may be positive in trichomoniasis).
  8. 8Spotted fish + looking for clues: Wet mount shows clue cells: vaginal epithelial cells covered with Gardnerella, giving a stippled or granular border; they may look bluish-purple with Gram or Giemsa stain.⭐ Clue cells are pathognomonic of BV.
  9. 9Metro: BV is treated with metronidazole or clindamycin.⭐ Metronidazole: avoid alcohol (disulfiram-like reaction).

6. Neisseria gonorrhoeae

Exam snapshot
  • Clinical clue: sexually active patient with purulent urethral or cervical discharge, or migratory polyarthralgia, tenosynovitis and pustular rash (disseminated infection); neonatal conjunctivitis within days of birth.
  • Micro: gram-negative diplococci inside neutrophils, oxidase positive, grows on Thayer-Martin, no capsule, IgA protease and pilus antigenic variation.
  • Diagnosis: NAAT (urine or swab); Gram stain and culture on Thayer-Martin.
  • Treatment/prevention: ceftriaxone plus empiric treatment for chlamydia coinfection (doxycycline); condoms; neonatal prophylaxis; test and treat partners.
  • Buzzwords: intracellular diplococci, PID, Fitz-Hugh-Curtis violin-string adhesions, disseminated gonococcal infection, ophthalmia neonatorum.
  1. 1Gonzo's Bar: N. gonorrhoeae are gram-negative diplococci and the cause of gonorrhea.
  2. 22 red cherries in mixed drink: N. gonorrhoeae diplococci are found within polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNs) on Gram stain (they survive intracellularly).⭐ Gram-negative intracellular diplococci in PMNs.
  3. 3Broken cake capsule: N. gonorrhoeae has no capsule (unlike N. meningitidis).⭐ Lack of capsule explains why there is no vaccine and re-infection is possible.
  4. 4Embracing couple: Gonorrhea is a reportable sexually transmitted infection (STI).
  5. 5Care-free workers: N. gonorrhoeae can reside in asymptomatic carriers (especially women and pharyngeal or rectal infection), allowing silent spread.
  6. 6Blindfolded baby cherub: N. gonorrhoeae can be transmitted to the newborn in the birth canal, causing ophthalmia neonatorum within about 5 days of birth; the infection is typically purulent and severe. Chlamydia trachomatis also causes neonatal conjunctivitis at 5-14 days, usually milder and less purulent.⭐ Gonococcal conjunctivitis appears earlier (day 2-5) than chlamydial (day 5-14).
  7. 7Leaky tap: Gonorrhea can cause urethritis that is often asymptomatic but may show WBCs in urine (sterile pyuria).
  8. 8Flaming milk bomb: Symptomatic gonococcal urethritis causes dysuria and purulent urethral discharge.
  9. 9Apple seat cushion: Ascending gonococcal urethritis can involve the prostate, causing prostatitis.
  10. 10Orchid: Ascending infection can involve the epididymis and testis, causing epididymo-orchitis.
  11. 11Neck of chandelier leaking candle wax: Gonorrhea can cause mucopurulent cervicitis.
  12. 12Dripping red wax between calendar dates: Intermenstrual bleeding, such as postcoital spotting, is a warning symptom of gonococcal cervicitis.
  13. 13Arms of chandelier leaking candle wax: Ascending infection causes pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), including salpingitis and tubo-ovarian abscess, which can lead to ectopic pregnancy and infertility from tubal scarring.⭐ PID is a leading cause of ectopic pregnancy and infertility.
  14. 14Fitz, Hugh, & Curtis Trio: Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome, a complication of PID, is perihepatitis in which infection spreads to the liver capsule and peritoneum, causing right upper quadrant pain.
  15. 15Violin strings: Perihepatic inflammation leads to thin, string-like adhesions between the liver capsule and the abdominal wall ("violin string" adhesions).⭐ Violin-string adhesions = Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome (also seen with chlamydia).
  16. 16Cracked piano leg: Disseminated gonococcal infection includes purulent arthritis (septic arthritis, often a single large joint).⭐ Most common cause of septic arthritis in sexually active young adults.
  17. 17Broken bass heel string: Disseminated gonococcal infection includes tenosynovitis, commonly of the tendons of the hands, wrists or ankles (e.g. Achilles).
  18. 18Red suits: Disseminated gonococcal infection includes dermatitis (pustular or vesiculopustular skin lesions).⭐ Triad: tenosynovitis, dermatitis, migratory polyarthralgia.
  19. 19Gnats: Nucleic acid amplification testing (NAAT) is the test of choice for diagnosing N. gonorrhoeae.
  20. 203 axes in Gonzo's bar: Ceftriaxone is the drug of choice for gonorrhea (third-generation cephalosporin).⭐ Add doxycycline for presumptive chlamydia coinfection.
  21. 21Clam lady + Cycle + Crow: Treat for presumptive Chlamydia trachomatis coinfection with doxycycline (or a macrolide such as azithromycin).
  22. 22Protective equipment: Counsel patients on barrier methods, such as consistent condom use, to prevent transmission.

7. Chlamydia trachomatis, Chlamydophila pneumoniae, Chlamydophila psittaci

  1. 1Clams: Chlamydia spp. are gram-indeterminate bacteria with three clinically significant species: Chlamydia trachomatis (serovars D-K, L1-L3, and A-C), Chlamydophila pneumoniae, and Chlamydophila psittaci, each with distinct clinical presentations.
  2. 2Ship on island: Chlamydia spp. are obligate intracellular pathogens, requiring a host cell to replicate.
  3. 3White island: Chlamydia spp. exhibit weak gram-negative staining due to their unique cell wall structures and are often classified as gram-indeterminate.
  4. 4"No mermaids": The cell wall of Chlamydia spp. lacks muramic acid, a typical component of peptidoglycan.
  5. 5Pearls outside of clam: Elementary bodies are the infectious, non-replicating, extracellular form of Chlamydia, formed in the first of two developmental stages.
  6. 6Pearls inside of clam: Reticulate bodies are the intracellular, replicating form of Chlamydia, formed in the second of two developmental stages.

8. Treponema pallidum

Exam snapshot
  • Clinical clue: painless genital chancre (primary), palm and sole rash/condyloma lata (secondary), gummas, aortitis, tabes dorsalis, Argyll Robertson pupils (tertiary); stillborn/rhinitis/Hutchinson teeth (congenital)
  • Spirochete, too thin for Gram stain, not culturable; visualized by dark-field microscopy
  • Dx: nontreponemal screen (VDRL/RPR) then confirm with treponemal test (FTA-ABS, TP-PA); VDRL titer tracks treatment response
  • Treatment: IM benzathine penicillin G for all stages (IV penicillin G for neurosyphilis); desensitize if allergic, especially in pregnancy
  • Buzzwords: chancre, condyloma lata, tree-barking aorta, Argyll Robertson pupil, saber shins, Hutchinson teeth, Jarisch-Herxheimer
  1. 1Pale spiral galaxy: Treponema pallidum is a spirochete that causes the sexually transmitted infection syphilis.
  2. 2Spiral staircase: T. pallidum is a spiral-shaped spirochete that causes syphilis.
  3. 3"Darkfield galaxy": Dark-field microscopy allows direct visualization of T. pallidum, but it needs a sample taken directly from a lesion and is not commonly used.⭐ Used for chancre or condyloma lata fluid; not for routine screening.
  4. 4"VDRL" on screen: VDRL is a nontreponemal test that detects antibodies to cardiolipin-lecithin-cholesterol complexes; it is a convenient, nonspecific screening test that can give false positives. RPR is another nontreponemal test that detects the same antibodies and is used for screening and for monitoring treatment response.⭐ Nontreponemal titers fall with successful treatment.
  5. 5"MONO, RF, SLE, LEP, DRG": Conditions that can cause a false-positive VDRL include mononucleosis, rheumatic fever (RF), systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), leprosy and drugs (IV drug use).⭐ Mnemonic: MONO, RF, SLE, LEP, DRG (also pregnancy and other viral infections).
  6. 6"Field Telescope A-Ab": A positive VDRL/RPR is usually followed by a specific treponemal test, such as the FTA-ABS (fluorescent treponemal antibody absorption) test, to confirm syphilis.⭐ Treponemal tests (FTA-ABS) usually remain positive for life.
  7. 71st level: Syphilis is divided into early (primary and secondary), latent (asymptomatic) and late (tertiary) stages, each with distinct clinical manifestations; this level represents primary syphilis.
  8. 82nd level: Syphilis is divided into early (primary and secondary), latent and late (tertiary) stages; this level represents secondary syphilis.
  9. 9Late night outside: Syphilis is divided into early (primary and secondary), latent and late (tertiary) stages; the outside represents late (tertiary) syphilis.
  10. 10Poked in butt + 1st level: In primary syphilis, a painless chancre appears at the site of inoculation (genitals or anus); it heals spontaneously in about 3-6 weeks.⭐ Painless, indurated ulcer with clean base, unlike painful chancroid.
  11. 11Solar system model + 2nd level: Secondary syphilis is a systemic disease with rash, fever, lymphadenopathy and mucous membrane lesions.
  12. 12Red gloves and boots + 2nd level: A widespread maculopapular rash that often involves the palms and soles is characteristic of secondary syphilis.⭐ Rash on palms and soles: secondary syphilis, RMSF, coxsackie.
  13. 13Planet with a lot of bumps + 2nd level: Condyloma lata are flat-topped, moist, highly infectious lesions of secondary syphilis on the genitals, perineum, perianal region, axillae and inner thighs.⭐ Not to be confused with HPV condyloma acuminata.
  14. 14Looking at planet with a lot of bumps through telescopes: Live T. pallidum spirochetes can be seen within condyloma lata by dark-field microscopy.
  15. 15Cratered moon + outside: Gummas are noninfectious, soft tumor-like lesions with firm necrotic centers that form in tertiary syphilis from chronic granulomatous inflammation; they can occur in skin, bone, liver or other organs and cause local tissue destruction.
  16. 16Aortic tree + outside: Syphilitic aortitis in tertiary syphilis produces "tree-barking" of the aorta and can lead to an ascending aortic (aortic root) aneurysm.⭐ Aortic root dilation can lead to aortic regurgitation.
  17. 17Tree roots + outside: Tertiary syphilitic aortitis is caused by endarteritis and destruction of the vasa vasorum (small vessels supplying the aortic wall) by T. pallidum.
  18. 18Damaged posterior columns + outside: Tabes dorsalis, a form of late neurosyphilis, is degeneration of the dorsal columns and dorsal roots, causing impaired proprioception and vibration sense, progressive sensory ataxia, lancinating pains and loss of deep tendon reflexes.⭐ Wide-based gait, positive Romberg, and Charcot joints.

  22. 22Saddle + outside: Saddle nose, collapse of the nasal bridge from cartilage destruction, is a feature of late congenital syphilis.⭐ Early congenital syphilis may present with snuffles (rhinitis).
  23. 23Chattering teeth + outside: Hutchinson teeth (blunted, notched incisors) and mulberry molars (abnormal enamel on molar cusps) are features of late congenital syphilis.
  24. 24Earmuffs + outside: Congenital syphilis can cause sensorineural hearing loss in both early (birth to 2 years) and late (after 2 years) stages.⭐ Hutchinson triad: notched incisors, interstitial keratitis, 8th nerve deafness.

  26. 26Purple pencil + 2nd level: Intramuscular benzathine penicillin G is the preferred treatment for secondary syphilis; in allergic patients, desensitize.
  27. 27Purple pencil + outside: Benzathine penicillin G is the preferred treatment for late (tertiary) syphilis, with desensitization in allergic patients; neurosyphilis requires IV aqueous penicillin G.

  29. 29Fiery + icy comet: The Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction causes fever and chills, sometimes headache, myalgia and worsening of the rash, within hours of treatment.⭐ Supportive care and antipyretics; not a penicillin allergy.
