1. Herpes Simplex Virus Types 1 & 2 (Herpesviridae)

  1. 1Hermes sprinting: Herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) and herpes simplex virus type 2 (HSV-2) (DNA viruses in the Herpesviridae family); they are highly contagious, and spread mainly via direct contact with infected skin, mucosa, or body fluids, as well as vertically
  2. 2Cool colors: HSV-1 and HSV-2 are DNA viruses
  3. 3Enveloping toga: HSV-1 and HSV-2 are enveloped viruses
  4. 4Double road lines: HSV-1 and HSV-2 are double-stranded, linear DNA viruses
  5. 5Cow spots: Intranuclear eosinophilic inclusions, known as Cowdry type A bodies, are characteristic histopathological findings in cells infected with HSV (as well as CMV and VZV); these cells typically show a Clear nuclear halo, multinucleation, nuclear molding, and chromatin margination, usually in keratinocytes or neurons
  6. 6Torch: HSV-1 and HSV-2 are part of the TORCHeS group of infections—T: Toxoplasma; O: Other (varicella-zoster virus, parvovirus B19, Zika virus, HIV); R: Rubella; C: Cytomegalovirus (CMV); H: Herpes simplex virus (HSV); S: Syphilis—a group of infections that are transmitted vertically through the placenta
  7. 7Red lips: The primary infection of HSV-1 often manifests as gingivostomatitis, characterized by painful vesicular lesions and ulcerations within the oral cavity and surrounding perioral region
  8. 8Lip ulcers: Herpes labialis (cold sores) presents as clusters of small, painful vesicles on a red base along the lip margin (vermilion border) that may rupture into shallow ulcers and heal with a yellow crust; most commonly caused by HSV-1
  9. 9Serpent caduceus: Keratoconjunctivitis, primarily attributable to HSV-1, presents with ocular pain, photophobia, redness, tearing, and decreased visual acuity; fluorescein slit-lamp examination, branching (dendritic or "serpiginous') corneal ulcers are diagnostic for herpes infection.
  10. 10Red wings on helmet near temples: HSV-1 encephalitis, the most common cause of sporadic encephalitis in the U.S., causes hemorrhagic temporal lobe necrosis, leading to fever, seizures, altered mental status, personality changes, and olfactory hallucinations due to limbic system involvement
  11. 11Black helmet HSV-1 encephalitis, the most common cause of sporadic encephalitis in the U.S., causes hemorrhagic temporal lobe necrosis, leading to fever, seizures, altered mental status, personality changes, and olfactory hallucinations due to limbic system involvement
  12. 12Crazy eyes: HSV-1 encephalitis, the most common cause of sporadic encephalitis in the U.S., causes hemorrhagic temporal lobe necrosis, leading to fever, seizures, altered mental status, personality changes, and olfactory hallucinations due to limbic system involvement
  13. 13Three helmet gems: Latent HSV-1 in the trigeminal ganglia can be reactivated by stress, sunlight, fever, or immunosuppression, leading to cold sores, keratoconjunctivitis, or, rarely, temporal lobe encephalitis
  14. 14Dew drops on roses: HSV lesions appear as painful grouped vesicles or pustules on an erythematous base and are sometimes likened to "dew drops on a rose petal"; they may rupture to form shallow ulcers, crust over as they heal, and recur due to viral latency
  15. 15Dew drops on finger: Herpetic whitlow is an HSV-induced condition characterized by painful, clustered vesicular lesions on an erythematous base on one or more fingers
  16. 16Target stamps on hand and arm: Erythema multiforme is a hypersensitivity reaction associated with certain medications and infections (such as HSV) that presents with distinct target lesions, with a central dusky area, pale edematous zone, and outer erythematous ring, on the back of the hands and feet and move centrally
  17. 17Ruffles by groin: During an initial genital herpes outbreak, often caused by HSV-2, patients may present with painful, grouped vesicles on an erythematous base in the genital or perianal region, often with shallow ulcers and tender inguinal lymphadenopathy
  18. 18Codpiece: HSV-2 primarily establishes latency in the sacral ganglia, and reactivation can cause recurrent genital or perianal lesions
  19. 19Neck brace: HS\V-2 infections have been linked to cases of aseptic meningitis in both adolescent and adult populations, particularly during primary genital infection, presenting with headache, fever, neck stiffness, and lymphocytic pleocytosis in adolescents and adults
  20. 20Tank + giant spots: The Tzanck smear was used historically to visualize multinucleated giant cells infected with HSV; however, multinucleated giant cells can also be seen in other conditions like VZV infections, and specific identification requires other tests, such as PCR or viral culture
  21. 21Violet recycling bin: Prophylactic measures against HSV outbreaks include the use of valacyclovir and acyclovir

4. Giardia lamblia

  2. 2Poopy water: Giardia intestinalis cysts (the infectious stage of the parasite) are transmitted via the fecal-oral route, often through contaminated water or food
  3. 3Bubbles in water: Giardia intestinalis forms infectious cysts, which are shed in the feces of infected individuals, contaminating water and food sources
  4. 4Backpacks: Giardiasis is a concern for individuals consuming water from natural sources, such as hikers and campers, due to the risk of ingesting contaminated water
  5. 5Water bottle + poopy water: Poorly purified or untreated water is a common vehicle for Giardia intestinalis transmission

  9. 9Shields in water: Detection of Giardia intestinalis cysts or trophozoites in stool specimens confirms a giardiasis diagnosis
  10. 10"O&P": Stool ova and parasite (O&P) examination and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) for Giardia antigens are reliable methods for diagnosing giardiasis

  2. 2Bubbles in water: Entamoeba histolytica exists in two main forms: the trophozoite, which is active, motile, and invasive, and the cyst, which is infectious, environmentally resistant, and passed through stool
