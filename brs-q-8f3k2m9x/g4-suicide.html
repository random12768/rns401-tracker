Notes on the study guide for this topic

Places where the guide is simplified, inconsistent or out of date. Use the standard textbook version for the exam where they differ.

  • The guide teaches SAD PERSONS as a risk tool; it is a teaching mnemonic that performs poorly as a predictive instrument, and current practice uses structured risk formulation (for example the Columbia scale) rather than scoring.
  • The guide states a previous attempt confers '100x' risk within a year and that lethal means access increases risk '3x'; these figures vary by study and population and should not be treated as exact.
  • The involuntary hospitalization box lists 'lacks capacity' as a general criterion; in most U.S. states commitment is based on danger to self or others (or grave disability) from mental illness, and lack of capacity is not itself required.
  • The guide says elderly white males over 75 have the highest completed suicide rate; this is correct for rate by age and sex group, whereas the greatest absolute number of deaths is in middle-aged adults.
