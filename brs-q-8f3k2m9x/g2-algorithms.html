Notes on the study guide for this topic

Places where the guide is simplified, inconsistent or out of date. Use the standard textbook version for the exam where they differ.

  • The guide labels atomoxetine an 'SNRI'; it is a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (NRI), and its boxed warning is for suicidal ideation in children and adolescents.
  • The guide describes clonidine/guanfacine as 'presynaptic alpha-2 agonism, decreased NE release'; guanfacine's ADHD benefit is attributed mainly to postsynaptic alpha-2A stimulation in the prefrontal cortex (clonidine acts more presynaptically).
  • The enuresis flowchart calls the alarm the '1st-line pharmacologic next step'; the alarm is a behavioral (conditioning) device, not a drug. The guide also lists 'Tetrabenazine' and 'typical antipsychotics' in the Tourette ladder, whereas current guidance favors aripiprazole or risperidone before haloperidol, and the guide's Tourette ladder says stimulants are used for comorbid ADHD, while current evidence shows stimulants generally do not worsen tics.
  • The guide states preschool ADHD (<6 years) should receive behavioral therapy alone; current AAP guidance applies this to ages 4 to 5, with methylphenidate considered if behavioral interventions fail and disturbance is moderate to severe.
