Childhood-onset schizophrenia and psychosis in children

Children & Adolescents · 5 NBME-style questions · pick an answer, then check it to see the full reasoning

Notes on the study guide for this topic

Places where the guide is simplified, inconsistent or out of date. Use the standard textbook version for the exam where they differ.

  • The guide gives atypical antipsychotics (risperidone, aripiprazole) as treatment but omits clozapine for treatment-resistant childhood-onset schizophrenia (failure of two adequate antipsychotic trials), which is the standard escalation step.
  • The guide does not mention that psychotic symptoms in a child should prompt exclusion of medical and substance causes (for example anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis, Wilson disease, epilepsy, drug intoxication) before diagnosing a primary psychotic disorder.
  • The guide states childhood-onset schizophrenia is the 'same disorder' as adult schizophrenia; this is broadly accepted, but childhood-onset cases show more insidious onset, more premorbid developmental abnormalities, and worse prognosis, and DSM-5-TR requires 6 months of disturbance.
