1. Bisphosphonates, Raloxifene, Denosumab, Calcitonin

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate, ibandronate, zoledronate, pamidronate), raloxifene (SERM), denosumab (anti-RANKL mAb), calcitonin; teriparatide is anabolic (PTH analog).
  • Mechanism: bisphosphonates - pyrophosphate analogs that bind hydroxyapatite and induce osteoclast apoptosis; raloxifene - estrogen agonist in bone; denosumab - blocks RANKL; calcitonin - inhibits osteoclasts.
  • Indications: postmenopausal and glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis, Paget disease, hypercalcemia of malignancy, bone metastases.
  • Adverse effects: bisphosphonates - esophagitis, osteonecrosis of jaw, atypical femur fractures, hypocalcemia; raloxifene - DVT/PE, hot flashes; denosumab - hypocalcemia, infections.
  • Buzzwords: take alendronate fasting with water, stay upright 30 minutes; raloxifene lowers breast cancer risk; RANKL-RANK-OPG axis; PTH continuous causes resorption, pulsatile builds bone.
  1. 1Fresh piece of calcified chalk: Osteoporosis therapy: bisphosphonates, SERMs (raloxifene), denosumab and calcitonin inhibit bone resorption, modestly increasing bone mineral density and reducing fractures (teriparatide is anabolic).
  2. 2Osteo-builders: Osteoblasts build bone (secrete osteoid, express RANKL, ALP).
  3. 3Destructive class: Osteoclasts resorb bone (derived from monocyte/macrophage lineage; activated via RANKL-RANK).
  4. 4DONATE: "-dronate" suffix: bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate, ibandronate, zoledronate, pamidronate) are first-line for osteoporosis.
  5. 5Two P coins: Bisphosphonates are structural analogs of pyrophosphate (P-C-P backbone, resistant to hydrolysis).
  6. 6Large T-rex appetite: Bisphosphonates bind avidly to hydroxyapatite at sites of active bone remodeling.
  7. 7Classmate stuck in donation box: Osteoclasts take up bisphosphonate during resorption, which impairs their adherence (ruffled border) and function.
  8. 8Class waiting to enter: Osteoclast precursors are recruited and differentiate under RANKL/M-CSF signalling.
  9. 9Preventing class from entering: Bisphosphonates decrease osteoclast precursor development and recruitment.
  10. 10Classmate's popping balloon: Bisphosphonates induce osteoclast apoptosis (nitrogen-containing ones inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase).
  11. 11Elevated calci-yum icecream: Bisphosphonates (zoledronate, pamidronate) treat acute hypercalcemia by inhibiting bone resorption.
  12. 12Massive calcified rock with metastatic crab fossils: Hypercalcemia of malignancy (PTHrP, osteolytic metastases) is treated acutely with IV fluids, calcitonin and bisphosphonates.
  13. 13Disorganized bone display: Paget disease of bone (excess osteoclast resorption then disorganized bone) is treated with bisphosphonates; calcitonin is an alternative.⭐ Alkaline phosphatase is elevated in Paget disease.
  14. 14Corroded neck: Oral bisphosphonates cause esophagitis, esophageal ulcers and reflux; take with a full glass of water, upright for 30 minutes, empty stomach; avoid in esophageal disorders.
  15. 15Crumbling jaw bone: Bisphosphonates (and denosumab) rarely cause osteonecrosis of the jaw (especially high-dose IV, with dental procedures) and atypical femoral fractures.
  16. 16Falling calci-yum icecream: Calcitonin lowers serum calcium and can cause hypocalcemia.
  17. 17Falling calci-yum icecream: Bisphosphonates can cause hypocalcemia (correct vitamin D and calcium deficiency first); denosumab does as well.
  18. 18Female symbol: Estrogen replacement prevents postmenopausal bone loss but is not first line because of risks (breast cancer, VTE, stroke, endometrial cancer if unopposed).
  19. 19Female guarding class entrance: Estrogen inhibits osteoclast differentiation and promotes osteoclast apoptosis; estrogen loss at menopause drives bone loss.
  20. 20Relax: Raloxifene is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) used to treat and prevent postmenopausal osteoporosis.
  21. 21Relaxing the waiting classmates: Raloxifene is an agonist in bone (inhibiting osteoclast differentiation), but an antagonist in breast and uterus (reducing breast cancer risk, no endometrial hyperplasia); adverse effects include hot flashes, VTE/DVT and stroke.⭐ Raloxifene: bone and lipids yes; endometrium no, but VTE risk.
  22. 22PthD: Parathyroid hormone (PTH) has dual effects: continuous elevation causes net bone resorption, while intermittent dosing (teriparatide) is anabolic.
  23. 23Convincing osteo-builder to give crank-drill: PTH stimulates osteoblasts and stromal cells to express RANKL (and M-CSF), which activates osteoclasts.
  24. 24Crank-drill: RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand) is the osteoblast-derived signal that drives osteoclast formation and activation; osteoprotegerin (OPG) is its decoy receptor.
  25. 25Active classmate with crank-drill: RANKL binding RANK on osteoclasts and precursors increases their differentiation and activity.
  26. 26Dino suit man grabbing crank-drill: Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody against RANKL, inhibiting osteoclast formation and function; treats osteoporosis (SC every 6 months) and bone metastases.⭐ Stopping denosumab causes rebound fractures; hypocalcemia risk.
  27. 27Antibody spikes: Denosumab is a human IgG2 monoclonal antibody ("-mab").
  28. 28Curator toning it down: Calcitonin (secreted by thyroid parafollicular C cells) "tones down" calcium by inhibiting resorption; limited efficacy in osteoporosis, weaker than bisphosphonates.
  29. 29Curator grabbing classmate: Calcitonin directly inhibits osteoclast activity (binding calcitonin receptors), decreasing bone resorption; also analgesic for acute vertebral fractures.
  30. 30Calci-yum ice cream pouring down flank: Calcitonin promotes renal calcium excretion, lowering serum calcium.

2. Teriparatide, Vitamin D, Cinacalcet, Sevelamer

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: teriparatide (PTH 1-34), abaloparatide, vitamin D (cholecalciferol, ergocalciferol, calcitriol, paricalcitol), cinacalcet (calcimimetic), sevelamer (phosphate binder).
  • Mechanism: intermittent PTH is anabolic to bone; calcitriol increases intestinal and renal Ca/P absorption; cinacalcet activates the parathyroid calcium-sensing receptor to lower PTH; sevelamer binds intestinal phosphate.
  • Indications: severe osteoporosis (teriparatide), rickets/osteomalacia/hypoparathyroidism/CKD (vitamin D), secondary hyperparathyroidism (cinacalcet), hyperphosphatemia in CKD (sevelamer).
  • Adverse effects: hypercalcemia (teriparatide, vitamin D), osteosarcoma warning with teriparatide, hypocalcemia and QT prolongation (cinacalcet), GI upset (sevelamer).
  • Buzzwords: pulsatile PTH builds bone, continuous PTH resorbs bone; CKD means low calcitriol, high phosphate, high PTH; Chvostek and Trousseau signs indicate hypocalcemia.
  1. 1Osteo-builders: Osteoblasts build bone; their function and maturation are stimulated by intermittent teriparatide and by vitamin D (calcitriol), increasing bone mineral density.
  2. 2Destructive classmates: Osteoclasts resorb bone; they are activated indirectly by PTH and calcitriol via osteoblast-derived RANKL, increasing bone resorption and turnover.
  3. 3Released calcified bones: Osteoclast activity releases calcium from bone into the circulation.
  4. 4Released P fossil: Osteoclast resorption also releases phosphate from bone.
  5. 5PthD paleontologist: Parathyroid hormone (PTH) raises serum calcium and lowers serum phosphate.
  6. 6PthD lab: The parathyroid glands (chief cells) secrete PTH.
  7. 7Calcified bone receiving: The calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) on parathyroid chief cells senses extracellular calcium (a Gq-coupled receptor).
  8. 8PthD stuck behind bones: High serum calcium activates CaSR and inhibits PTH synthesis and secretion; low calcium stimulates PTH.⭐ Negative feedback: high Ca2+ suppresses PTH; high phosphate and low calcitriol raise it.
  9. 9PthD convincing osteo-builder to give up crank-drill: PTH binds PTH1 receptors on osteoblasts, stimulating release of RANKL (and M-CSF), which activates osteoclasts.
  10. 10Classmate receiving crank-drill: RANKL binds RANK on osteoclasts and precursors, increasing differentiation and bone resorption.
  11. 11PthD teaching osteo-builders: Continuous (high-level) PTH exposure directly stimulates osteoblasts but indirectly activates osteoclasts, producing net bone resorption.
  12. 12PthD gathering bones and dropping P fossils: In the kidney, PTH increases calcium reabsorption (distal tubule) and decreases phosphate reabsorption in the proximal tubule (phosphaturia).
  13. 131-head added to Calci-TRON: Renal 1-alpha-hydroxylase converts 25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcidiol) to 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol), the active form.
  14. 14PthD adding final piece to Calci-TRON: PTH upregulates 1-alpha-hydroxylase, increasing calcitriol production.
  15. 15PthD teaching assistant (TA): Teriparatide is recombinant PTH 1-34 (abaloparatide is a PTHrP analog), given by daily SC injection.
  16. 16TA teaching osteo-builders: Intermittent (daily pulsatile) teriparatide stimulates osteoblast maturation and bone formation, an anabolic effect, in contrast to continuous PTH which causes resorption.⭐ Pulsatile PTH builds bone; continuous PTH resorbs bone.
  17. 17Fresh piece of calcified chalk: Teriparatide treats severe osteoporosis (increases bone density and decreases vertebral fractures), especially in high fracture risk or after bisphosphonate failure.
  18. 18TA gathering bones and dropping P fossils: Like PTH, teriparatide increases renal calcium reabsorption and phosphate excretion.
  19. 19TA adding final piece to Calci-TRON: Teriparatide increases 1-alpha-hydroxylase activity and calcitriol synthesis.
  20. 20Solar D3 battery: Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) comes from UVB conversion of 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin and from animal sources (dairy, fish).
  21. 21Earth-friendly D2 battery: Vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol) comes from plant sources and supplements.
  22. 22Robot body added to D battery in liver-barrow: Hepatic 25-hydroxylase converts vitamin D to 25-hydroxyvitamin D (calcidiol), the storage form measured clinically.
  23. 23Calci-TRON gathering bones and fossils from dump site: Calcitriol increases renal reabsorption of calcium and phosphate.
  24. 24Calci-TRON gathering bones and fossils from GI truck: Calcitriol increases intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate (via calbindin), the main way it raises serum calcium.
  25. 25Calci-TRONI delivering crank-drills: Calcitriol stimulates osteoblasts to release RANKL, activating osteoclasts and mobilizing calcium and phosphate from bone.
  26. 26Calci-TRON collapsing PthD lab: Calcitriol inhibits PTH synthesis by the parathyroid gland (negative feedback); used in secondary hyperparathyroidism of CKD.
  27. 27Calci-TRON teaching osteo-builders: Calcitriol also promotes osteoblast maturation and bone mineralization (increased bone formation).
  28. 28Fresh piece of calcified chalk: Vitamin D (with calcium) is used for prevention and treatment of osteoporosis, particularly in deficiency.
  29. 29Calci-TRON stabilizing rickety tower: Vitamin D treats rickets (children) and osteomalacia (adults) by restoring bone mineralization.
  30. 30Calci-TRON stabilizing broken kidney: In chronic kidney disease, the kidney cannot make calcitriol (low 1-alpha-hydroxylase), so calcitriol or analogs (paricalcitol) prevent hypocalcemia and secondary hyperparathyroidism.
  31. 31Scaly knee and elbow pads: Topical vitamin D analogs (calcipotriene) treat plaque psoriasis by inhibiting keratinocyte proliferation.
  32. 32Calci-TRON saving falling calcified bones: Calcitriol is used for long-term management of hypocalcemia, such as hypoparathyroidism (the OCR's hypothyroidism likely means hypoparathyroidism).check original
  33. 33Falling PthD: Hypocalcemia is commonly caused by hypoparathyroidism, with decreased PTH leading to decreased renal calcitriol production, calcium reabsorption and bone resorption.
  34. 34Undone bowtie on PthD: Thyroid surgery (thyroidectomy) can damage or remove parathyroid glands, causing hypoparathyroidism and postoperative hypocalcemia.
  35. 35Shaking structure: Severe hypocalcemia can cause seizures and prolonged QT.
  36. 36Tense fist: Hypocalcemia increases neuromuscular excitability: perioral/acral paresthesias, muscle cramps, laryngospasm, trismus and tetany (Chvostek and Trousseau signs).⭐ Chvostek = facial twitch; Trousseau = carpal spasm with BP cuff.
  37. 37Raised calci-yum ice cream: Teriparatide and vitamin D can cause hypercalcemia (stones, bones, groans, psychiatric overtones); teriparatide is also avoided in Paget disease, prior skeletal radiation and bone metastases because of osteosarcoma concern.
  38. 38Calculator at the calcified bone receptor: Cinacalcet, a calcimimetic, allosterically activates the parathyroid calcium-sensing receptor, increasing its sensitivity to calcium and decreasing PTH secretion.⭐ Adverse effects: hypocalcemia, nausea, QT prolongation.
  39. 39Calculating pile of calcified bones: Cinacalcet treats secondary hyperparathyroidism in CKD on dialysis, parathyroid carcinoma and refractory primary hyperparathyroidism hypercalcemia.
  40. 40Shoveling fossils in the GI truck: Sevelamer is a non-calcium phosphate-binding polymer that binds dietary phosphate in the gut, decreasing absorption (also lowers LDL).
  41. 41Shoveling pile of fossils: Sevelamer (and lanthanum, calcium acetate) treats hyperphosphatemia of chronic kidney disease, preventing secondary hyperparathyroidism and vascular calcification; GI upset is the main adverse effect.

3. Insulin, Sulfonylureas, Meglitinides, GLP-1 Agonists, DPP-4 Inhibitors

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: insulins (rapid: lispro/aspart/glulisine; short: regular; intermediate: NPH; long: glargine/detemir/degludec), sulfonylureas, meglitinides, GLP-1 agonists (-tide), DPP-4 inhibitors (-gliptin), metformin, TZDs, SGLT2 inhibitors (-gliflozin).
  • Mechanisms: insulin activates a receptor tyrosine kinase (GLUT4 insertion); sulfonylureas/meglitinides close beta-cell K-ATP channels; GLP-1 agonists mimic incretin; DPP-4 inhibitors prevent incretin breakdown; metformin reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis; TZDs are PPAR-gamma agonists; SGLT2i cause glycosuria.
  • Key indications: metformin first-line for type 2 DM; insulin for type 1, DKA, hyperkalemia (regular IV) and advanced type 2; GLP-1 agonist or SGLT2i preferred with ASCVD, HF or CKD.
  • Key adverse effects: hypoglycemia and weight gain (insulin, sulfonylureas); lactic acidosis and B12 deficiency (metformin); HF, edema, fractures (TZDs); genitourinary infections and euglycemic DKA (SGLT2i); pancreatitis and thyroid C-cell tumor warning (GLP-1 agonists).
  • Buzzwords/antidotes: C-peptide (high with secretagogues, low with exogenous insulin); chlorpropamide disulfiram-like reaction; treat hypoglycemia with glucose/dextrose or glucagon; check K+ before insulin in DKA; A1c goal below 7% for most adults.
  1. 1Welcome INSIDE mat: Insulin is the anabolic, storage hormone of the body, promoting uptake and storage of glucose, fat and amino acids.
  2. 2LangerHansel: The islets of Langerhans of the pancreas contain beta cells (insulin), alpha cells (glucagon) and delta cells (somatostatin).
  3. 3Beta-2 tuba: Pancreatic beta cells synthesize insulin; secretion is stimulated by glucose (main), amino acids, GLP-1/GIP incretins, vagal input and sympathetic beta-2 stimulation (alpha-2 stimulation inhibits it).
  4. 4LangerHansel's candy: Glucose is the most potent stimulant of insulin secretion; oral glucose gives a bigger response than IV via incretins.
  5. 5Closed gate around banana candy flowers: Glucose uptake (GLUT2) raises beta-cell ATP, which closes ATP-sensitive K+ channels (Kir6.2/SUR1).
  6. 6Gretel rushing in on calci-yum icecream flower: Closing the K+ channels depolarizes the beta cell, opening voltage-gated Ca2+ channels; Ca2+ influx triggers exocytosis of insulin.⭐ Sulfonylureas exploit this exact step.
  7. 7C wrapper: C-peptide is cleaved from proinsulin in secretory granules and released in equimolar amounts with insulin; it indicates endogenous insulin production (low in exogenous insulin administration, high in insulinoma or sulfonylurea use).⭐ Hypoglycemia with high C-peptide = insulinoma or sulfonylurea; low C-peptide = exogenous insulin.
  8. 8Tyrosine tire swing: The insulin receptor is a receptor tyrosine kinase; insulin binding autophosphorylates the receptor and activates IRS-1/PI3K-Akt and MAPK pathways.
  9. 94 on open door: Insulin promotes insertion of GLUT4 into the membrane of skeletal muscle and adipose tissue, increasing glucose uptake; brain, RBCs, and beta cells use insulin-independent transporters.
  10. 10Full liver candy jar: In the liver, insulin increases glycogenesis, glycolysis and lipogenesis and decreases glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis.
  11. 11Glycogen glazed ham: In muscle, insulin increases glycogen synthesis and protein synthesis (amino acid uptake) while decreasing proteolysis.
  12. 12Full fatty donut jar: In adipose tissue, insulin stimulates lipoprotein lipase, triglyceride storage and decreases hormone-sensitive lipase (lipolysis).
  13. 13Girls And Lads: Rapid-acting insulin analogs: Glulisine, Aspart, Lispro; given right before or with meals (prandial/bolus).
  14. 14Tall immediate peak: Rapid-acting analogs have onset about 15 minutes, peak 1-2 hours and short duration (3-5 hours).
  15. 15Birds nibbling the peak: These analogs blunt the postprandial glucose spike and are used for mealtime bolus and in insulin pumps.
  16. 16Rest Now: Regular insulin is short-acting; NPH (neutral protamine Hagedorn) is intermediate-acting.⭐ Regular insulin is the only IV insulin.
  17. 17Delayed peak: Regular insulin: onset 30-60 minutes, peak 2-4 hours, duration 5-8 hours; NPH: onset 1-2 hours, peak 4-12 hours, duration 12-18 hours (more delayed than regular).
  18. 18Ivy under "R": Regular insulin is the only formulation given intravenously (also SC) and is used in hospitalized patients and emergencies.
  19. 19Ivy next to eaten banana: IV regular insulin (with glucose to prevent hypoglycemia) is used to treat hyperkalemia by shifting K+ into cells.
  20. 20Candy key: Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a complication of insulin deficiency (type 1) with hyperglycemia, ketosis, anion-gap metabolic acidosis, vomiting, polyuria and Kussmaul respirations.
  21. 21Ivy next to candy key: DKA is treated with IV fluids, IV regular insulin infusion and electrolyte (K+) replacement; check K+ before insulin because insulin drives K+ into cells and can cause hypokalemia.⭐ Start insulin only when K+ is above 3.3 mEq/L.
  22. 22Eaten banana candy: Insulin lowers serum K+ by increasing Na+/K+ ATPase activity in skeletal muscle, driving K+ into cells.
  23. 23Don't Go: Long-acting insulin analogs: Detemir and Glargine (also degludec) provide basal insulin.
  24. 24Flat roof: Glargine and detemir (and degludec) have prolonged duration and a relatively flat (peakless) profile, providing basal coverage; glargine has no peak and should not be mixed with other insulins.
  25. 25Falling candy: Hypoglycemia is the most common adverse effect of insulin, presenting with adrenergic symptoms (tachycardia, palpitations, sweating, tremor, anxiety) and neuroglycopenia (confusion, seizure, coma).
  26. 26Sulfa egg laying swan: Sulfonylureas (glyburide, glipizide, glimepiride, tolbutamide) are structurally related to sulfonamides; cross-reactivity with sulfa-antibiotic allergy is rare but a labeled caution.
  27. 27Sulfa swan lake behind CLOSED banana gate: Sulfonylureas bind the SUR1 subunit of the beta-cell ATP-sensitive K+ channel, closing it, causing depolarization, Ca2+ influx and insulin release (insulin secretagogue).
  28. 28Mother swan in a maid outfit: 1st-generation sulfonylureas (tolbutamide, chlorpropamide, tolazamide; "-amide" names) are rarely used today; chlorpropamide has a very long duration of action.
  29. 29Goslings riding on mother's back: 2nd-generation sulfonylureas (glyburide, glipizide, glimepiride; "gli-" prefix) are more potent, require smaller doses and are more commonly used; glyburide and glimepiride are long-acting.
  30. 30Short zig-zagging gosling: Glipizide has the shortest duration of action of the 2nd-generation agents and a lower hypoglycemia risk; preferred in renal impairment and the elderly.⭐ Glyburide has active metabolites - avoid in renal failure.
  31. 31Gliding: "-glinide" suffix: meglitinides (repaglinide, nateglinide).
  32. 32Gliding goose behind CLOSED banana gate: Meglitinides close the beta-cell ATP-sensitive K+ channel at a different binding site on SUR1, stimulating short-acting insulin release; given before meals to cover postprandial glucose.
  33. 33C wrapper: Because they stimulate endogenous insulin secretion, sulfonylureas and meglitinides raise C-peptide levels along with insulin.
  34. 34Father gliding goose can't lay eggs: Meglitinides are not sulfonamide derivatives, so they can be used in patients with sulfa/sulfonylurea allergy.
  35. 35Falling candy: Sulfonylureas and meglitinides can cause hypoglycemia (more with long-acting sulfonylureas, especially with renal impairment, missed meals, or elderly patients).
  36. 36Fat old hag: Insulin secretagogues cause weight gain from increased insulin levels.
  37. 37"Do not drink" next to mother sulfonylurea swan: 1st-generation sulfonylureas, especially chlorpropamide, can cause a disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol (flushing, nausea); chlorpropamide also causes SIADH.
  38. 382 fingers: Sulfonylureas and meglitinides are used only for type 2 diabetes, because they require functioning beta cells.
  39. 39ExenaTIDE detergent: "-tide" suffix: GLP-1 receptor agonists (exenatide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, semaglutide).
  40. 40Gulp activated by hag: GLP-1 receptor agonists mimic the incretin GLP-1: they increase glucose-dependent insulin release, decrease glucagon release, slow gastric emptying and increase satiety (weight loss).⭐ Exenatide was derived from Gila monster saliva (exendin-4).
  41. 414 DRIPPING laundry items: DPP-4 inhibitors block dipeptidyl peptidase-4, the enzyme that degrades GLP-1 and GIP, prolonging their action.
  42. 42Clipped in clothespins: "-gliptin" suffix: DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin, saxagliptin, linagliptin, alogliptin).
  43. 43Endogenous gulps: DPP-4 inhibitors raise endogenous GLP-1 (and GIP) levels, increasing glucose-dependent insulin release, decreasing glucagon and gastric emptying (weight-neutral effects).
  44. 44Falling empty glucagon packets: GLP-1 agonists and DPP-4 inhibitors decrease glucagon secretion, lowering hepatic glucose output.
  45. 45Sealed gastric container: These agents slow gastric emptying, contributing to satiety and reduced postprandial glucose; GI side effects follow, particularly with GLP-1 agonists.
  46. 46Clothespin clipping nose: DPP-4 inhibitors may increase risk of nasopharyngitis and upper respiratory tract infections.
  47. 47Squeezing pancreas sponge: GLP-1 agonists (and DPP-4 inhibitors) are associated with acute pancreatitis; avoid in patients with a history of pancreatitis. GLP-1 agonists are also contraindicated with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2.⭐ Boxed warning: thyroid C-cell tumors with liraglutide/semaglutide.
  48. 48Candies NOT falling: GLP-1 agonists and DPP-4 inhibitors have a low risk of hypoglycemia because their insulin effect is glucose-dependent (risk rises when combined with sulfonylureas or insulin).
  49. 492 fingers: GLP-1 agonists and DPP-4 inhibitors are used for type 2 diabetes and need residual beta-cell function.
  50. 50C wrapper: These drugs increase endogenous insulin release and so raise C-peptide levels.

4. Metformin, Thiazolidinediones, Pramlintide, SGLT2 Inhibitors

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: metformin (biguanide), pioglitazone/rosiglitazone (TZDs), pramlintide (amylin analog), acarbose/miglitol (alpha-glucosidase inhibitors), canagliflozin/dapagliflozin/empagliflozin (SGLT2 inhibitors).
  • Mechanisms: metformin - AMPK activation, decreased hepatic gluconeogenesis; TZDs - PPAR-gamma agonists; pramlintide - decreases glucagon and gastric emptying; acarbose - blocks brush border disaccharidases; SGLT2i - block proximal tubule glucose reabsorption.
  • Indications: type 2 diabetes (metformin first-line; pramlintide also type 1); SGLT2i also for heart failure and CKD; metformin for PCOS.
  • Adverse effects: metformin - lactic acidosis, GI upset, B12 deficiency; TZDs - edema, HF, weight gain, fractures; pramlintide - severe hypoglycemia with insulin; acarbose - flatulence; SGLT2i - UTI, candidiasis, volume depletion, euglycemic DKA.
  • Buzzwords: no hypoglycemia as monotherapy (metformin, TZD, acarbose, SGLT2i); TZDs contraindicated in class III-IV HF; metformin contraindicated at eGFR below 30; hypoglycemia on acarbose needs glucose, not sucrose.
  1. 12nd grade: Metformin, thiazolidinediones, pramlintide, alpha-glucosidase inhibitors and SGLT2 inhibitors are used for type 2 diabetes (pramlintide also type 1); most need residual beta-cell function or act independently of endogenous insulin.
  2. 2Metaphor: Metformin, a biguanide, is the first-line oral agent for type 2 diabetes.
  3. 3Bitten mitochondrial candy: Metformin inhibits mitochondrial glycerophosphate dehydrogenase (and complex I), shifting redox state and decreasing hepatic gluconeogenesis.
  4. 4AMPKandy: Metformin activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), decreasing hepatic gluconeogenesis and increasing insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake in muscle.
  5. 5Stopping candy release from liver bag: The main effect of metformin is decreased hepatic glucose production (gluconeogenesis).⭐ Metformin works without stimulating insulin secretion, so no hypoglycemia.
  6. 6INSIDE open candy box: Metformin increases peripheral insulin sensitivity (reduces insulin resistance); it is useful in PCOS as well.
  7. 7Spilled sour milk: Metformin can cause lactic acidosis (rare, but a boxed warning) by inhibiting mitochondrial lactate metabolism.⭐ Contraindicated in severe renal impairment, hepatic disease, alcohol abuse.
  8. 8Cracked kidney tray: Renal insufficiency (eGFR below 30) decreases metformin clearance and increases risk of lactic acidosis.
  9. 9Nauseated: Metformin causes GI effects: anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort; long-term use can also cause vitamin B12 deficiency.
  10. 10Skinny kid: Metformin is weight neutral or causes modest weight loss.
  11. 11Glitter: "-glitazone" suffix: thiazolidinediones (pioglitazone, rosiglitazone).
  12. 12PPARy in circle: Thiazolidinediones are agonist ligands of PPAR-gamma, a nuclear receptor that regulates gene transcription of insulin-responsive genes, primarily in adipose tissue.
  13. 13Turtle neck: PPAR-gamma activation upregulates adiponectin (adipokine), increasing insulin sensitivity and fatty acid oxidation.
  14. 14Elevated fatty donut jar: TZDs promote adipocyte differentiation and increase the number of small, insulin-sensitive adipocytes.
  15. 15Eating fatty donut: TZDs increase fatty acid uptake and triglyceride storage in adipose tissue and promote fatty acid oxidation, lowering circulating free fatty acids and triglycerides.
  16. 164 on open door: TZDs upregulate GLUT4 expression, increasing peripheral glucose uptake.
  17. 17INSIDE open candy box: Net effect of TZDs: improved insulin sensitivity in muscle, fat and liver (less hepatic gluconeogenesis).⭐ Onset is slow (weeks).
  18. 18Fat belly: TZDs cause weight gain (fluid retention and increased subcutaneous fat).
  19. 19Baggy pants: TZDs cause fluid retention and peripheral edema (increased renal sodium reabsorption).
  20. 20Crushed failing heart balloon: TZD-induced fluid retention can precipitate or worsen heart failure; contraindicated in NYHA class III-IV heart failure.⭐ Rosiglitazone also carries increased MI risk; pioglitazone linked to bladder cancer.
  21. 21Fractured chair leg: TZDs reduce bone mineral density and increase risk of distal extremity fractures, especially in women.
  22. 22Amy and Lynn: Pramlintide is an injectable analog of amylin (islet amyloid polypeptide, co-secreted with insulin from beta cells); it decreases glucagon, slows gastric emptying and increases satiety.
  23. 23Closed gastric water cooler: Pramlintide slows gastric emptying, blunting postprandial glucose peaks.
  24. 24Falling empty glucagon packets: Pramlintide suppresses postprandial glucagon secretion.
  25. 251 and 2: Pramlintide is an adjunct to mealtime insulin in both type 1 and type 2 diabetes.
  26. 26Eaten cupcake: Pramlintide is given before meals to control the postprandial glucose spike.
  27. 27Falling candy: Pramlintide can cause severe hypoglycemia, especially when co-administered with insulin (reduce the insulin dose).⭐ Boxed warning: severe hypoglycemia.
  28. 28Nauseated: Pramlintide commonly causes nausea, vomiting and anorexia (weight loss).
  29. 29"Monosaccharide free": Inhibiting intestinal alpha-glucosidase prevents breakdown of disaccharides and oligosaccharides into absorbable monosaccharides.

5. ADH, DDAVP, ADH Receptor Antagonists

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: desmopressin (DDAVP), vasopressin (ADH); V2 antagonists (vaptans): conivaptan (IV), tolvaptan (oral); demeclocycline for SIADH.
  • Mechanism: ADH V1 (Gq) = vasoconstriction; V2 (Gs, cAMP) = aquaporin-2 insertion in collecting duct and release of vWF and factor VIII. DDAVP is V2-selective.
  • Indications: DDAVP for central DI, mild hemophilia A, von Willebrand disease (not type 3), nocturnal enuresis; vasopressin for septic shock/variceal bleeding; vaptans for SIADH hyponatremia.
  • Nephrogenic DI treated with thiazides, amiloride (lithium DI), NSAIDs. Adverse: DDAVP hyponatremia; vaptans overly rapid Na+ correction (osmotic demyelination), tolvaptan hepatotoxicity.
  • Buzzwords: central DI responds to DDAVP, nephrogenic DI does not; SIADH = euvolemic hyponatremia with high urine osmolality; '-vaptan' = V2 blocker; demeclocycline causes nephrogenic DI.
  1. 1Collecting duct: The collecting duct (principal cells) is the main renal site of action of antidiuretic hormone (ADH, vasopressin).
  2. 2Hydrating waterboy: ADH (vasopressin) is the hormone that retains free water; released in response to high plasma osmolality or hypovolemia.⭐ ADH is made in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus.
  3. 3Posterior water jug: ADH is synthesized in the hypothalamus but stored and released from the posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis).
  4. 4V1 receptor (Q-shaped green): ADH activates V1 receptors, which are Gq-coupled: phospholipase C -> IP3 + DAG -> increased intracellular Ca2+.
  5. 5Constricted golf club: V1 activation on vascular smooth muscle causes vasoconstriction and raises blood pressure (hence the name vasopressin).⭐ Vasopressin is used as a pressor in septic shock.
  6. 6V2 receptor (S-shaped green): ADH activates V2 receptors, which are Gs-coupled: adenylyl cyclase -> increased cAMP -> PKA.
  7. 7Translocation of pure water: V2 activation on the basolateral membrane of collecting duct principal cells causes insertion of aquaporin-2 channels into the apical membrane, so free water is reabsorbed and urine is concentrated.⭐ V2 = 2 = water (aquaporin-2); V1 = vasoconstriction.
  8. 8Endothelial tile: Extrarenal V2 receptors are found on vascular endothelium.
  9. 9von Willebrand pool table: V2 activation on vascular endothelium causes release of von Willebrand factor (vWF) from Weibel-Palade bodies.
  10. 108-ball: V2 activation on vascular endothelium also causes release of factor VIII (carried by vWF).
  11. 11Insipidus fountain: Diabetes insipidus (DI) = polyuria with dilute urine and polydipsia due to lack of ADH action; plasma osmolality and serum Na+ tend to be high.
  12. 12Dehydrating kidney sand trap: Nephrogenic DI: the kidney is resistant to ADH, so ADH levels are normal or high.
  13. 13Ignoring waterboy: Nephrogenic DI occurs when the collecting duct does not respond appropriately to ADH (V2 receptor or aquaporin-2 defect, or drug-induced).⭐ Water deprivation test: urine stays dilute after desmopressin in nephrogenic DI.
  14. 14Lift-ium balloons: Lithium is the classic drug cause of nephrogenic DI (enters principal cells via ENaC and impairs ADH signaling/aquaporin-2 expression).⭐ Other causes: demeclocycline, hypercalcemia, hypokalemia.
  15. 15Chloro-thighs: Thiazide diuretics treat nephrogenic DI: volume depletion increases proximal Na+/water reabsorption, so less fluid reaches the collecting duct and urine output falls (paradoxical effect).
  16. 16Almond cart (amiloride): Amiloride (K+-sparing ENaC blocker) treats nephrogenic DI, especially lithium-induced.
  17. 17Falling lift-ium balloon man: Amiloride treats lithium-induced DI by blocking ENaC entry of Li+ into collecting duct cells, reducing intracellular lithium.⭐ Amiloride is the drug of choice for lithium nephrogenic DI.
  18. 18Fire extinguisher: NSAIDs (e.g. indomethacin) treat nephrogenic DI by decreasing prostaglandin-mediated GFR/renal blood flow and prostaglandin antagonism of ADH, which reduces urine volume.
  19. 19Dehydrating brain sand trap: Central DI: the brain/pituitary fails to make or release enough ADH (e.g. head trauma, neurosurgery, tumor, hypophysitis).
  20. 20Absent waterboys: Central DI occurs when the hypothalamic-pituitary axis does not release adequate ADH; urine osmolality rises after desmopressin.
  21. 21Waterboy entering exogenously: Exogenous ADH replacement treats central DI.
  22. 22Desert-mobile: Desmopressin acetate (DDAVP), a long-acting synthetic ADH analog, is the treatment of choice for central DI (intranasal, oral, or IV/SC).
  23. 23V-2 engine: DDAVP is highly selective for the V2 receptor with minimal V1 pressor activity.⭐ Because it spares V1, DDAVP causes little vasoconstriction.
  24. 24Scratched von Willebrand felt: DDAVP treats von Willebrand disease (types 1 and some 2A) by releasing stored vWF from endothelium.⭐ DDAVP is ineffective in type 3 vWD (no vWF stores).
  25. 25Bruised with mucosal bleeding: Von Willebrand disease (deficient or defective vWF; most common inherited bleeding disorder) causes easy bruising and mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, menorrhagia); platelet-type bleeding with prolonged bleeding time.
  26. 26Missing 8-ball in A-frame: DDAVP treats mild hemophilia A by releasing factor VIII from vascular endothelium.
  27. 27Bleeding knee joint: Hemophilia A (X-linked recessive factor VIII deficiency) causes hemarthrosis and prolonged bleeding after minor procedures; PTT is prolonged with normal PT and bleeding time.
  28. 28Wet mattress: DDAVP treats nocturnal enuresis by decreasing nighttime urine production.
  29. 29Falling salty peanut shells: DDAVP can cause hyponatremia (water retention); counsel on fluid restriction.⭐ Hyponatremia and seizures are the main DDAVP toxicity.
  30. 30Bulging venous golf clubs: Vasopressin (or its analog terlipressin) is useful in esophageal variceal bleeding: V1-mediated splanchnic vasoconstriction lowers portal pressure.⭐ Octreotide is more commonly used first-line for variceal bleeding.
  31. 31Brain-shaped water hazard: Syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion (SIADH): euvolemic hyponatremia with concentrated urine; causes include small cell lung cancer, CNS disease, pulmonary disease, and drugs (carbamazepine, SSRIs, cyclophosphamide).
  32. 32Over-hydrating waterboy: SIADH is caused by excess ADH production or release, producing free water retention, low serum osmolality and inappropriately high urine osmolality.
  33. 33Vaporizer: The '-vaptan' suffix denotes ADH (vasopressin) receptor antagonists: conivaptan (IV, V1a/V2) and tolvaptan (oral, selective V2), used for SIADH and euvolemic or hypervolemic hyponatremia (e.g. heart failure).
  34. 34Wet pants: Vaptans block V2 and promote aquaresis (excretion of free water without electrolyte loss), correcting hyponatremia.
  35. 35Elevated peanuts hitting head: Overly rapid correction with vaptans can cause overcorrection (hypernatremia) and central pontine myelinolysis / osmotic demyelination (dysarthria, dysphagia, quadriparesis, locked-in state).⭐ Limit Na+ correction to under 8-10 mEq/L per 24 h; tolvaptan can also be hepatotoxic.
  36. 36Bicycle (demeclocycline): Demeclocycline, a tetracycline that induces nephrogenic DI, treats SIADH when fluid restriction fails (vaptans are an alternative); do not use in pregnancy or children (teeth/bone effects).

Exam snapshot

  • Adverse effects: Cushing syndrome, hyperglycemia, osteoporosis/AVN, skin thinning, myopathy, hypertension, hypokalemia, psychosis, cataracts, peptic ulcer, immunosuppression, adrenal suppression with abrupt withdrawal.

  3. 3Moon face: Glucocorticoid drugs mimic cortisol; the moon face represents both the drug class and its Cushingoid side effects.

  9. 9Inhibited lacrosse stick: Glucocorticoids reduce leukotriene production (lipoxygenase pathway) via decreased arachidonic acid substrate.

  23. 23Fainted druid: Acute adrenal insufficiency (adrenal crisis) causes hypotension, circulatory shock and death if untreated; precipitated by stress, infection or abrupt steroid withdrawal.
