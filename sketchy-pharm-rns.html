1. Asthma Therapy

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: SABA (albuterol, terbutaline), LABA (salmeterol, formoterol), inhaled corticosteroids (beclomethasone, budesonide, fluticasone), LTRA (montelukast, zafirlukast), zileuton, theophylline, cromolyn, omalizumab, ipratropium.
  • Mechanisms: beta-2 agonism raises cAMP (bronchodilation); steroids block inflammation; LTRAs block CysLT1; zileuton inhibits 5-lipoxygenase; theophylline inhibits PDE and blocks adenosine; omalizumab binds free IgE; cromolyn stabilizes mast cells.
  • Use: SABA for acute relief; ICS is the mainstay controller; add LABA (never alone), LTRA, theophylline or omalizumab; exacerbation: SABA, ipratropium, systemic steroids, epinephrine/terbutaline.
  • Adverse effects: SABA - tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia; ICS - oral candidiasis, dysphonia; zileuton - hepatotoxicity; theophylline - narrow therapeutic index, arrhythmia, seizures; montelukast - neuropsychiatric effects.
  • Buzzwords: aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (COX blockade shifts to leukotrienes; use LTRA), LTD4 most potent bronchoconstrictor, cromolyn is prophylaxis only, omalizumab for allergic IgE-mediated asthma.
  1. 1Dilated beta 2 tuba: Selective beta-2 agonists (albuterol, levalbuterol) activate Gs, increase cAMP and relax bronchial smooth muscle, relieving bronchoconstriction (rescue therapy).
  2. 2ROL call: The "-terol" suffix (albuterol, salmeterol, formoterol, pirbuterol) marks selective beta-2 agonists.
  3. 3"Do not disturb": Terbutaline is a selective beta-2 agonist that treats bronchoconstriction; it also relaxes the uterus (tocolysis, "do not disturb" the pregnancy) and is a subcutaneous option in severe asthma.⭐ Beta-2 agonists cause hypokalemia, tremor and tachycardia.
  4. 4Inhaler: Short-acting beta-2 agonists are delivered by metered-dose inhaler or nebulizer for acute symptom relief (rescue inhaler).
  5. 5Moon face: Inhaled corticosteroids (beclomethasone, budesonide, fluticasone) are first-line daily maintenance (controller) therapy for persistent asthma.
  6. 6Moon eclipsing inflammatory sun: Corticosteroids reduce airway inflammation by decreasing cytokine production, eosinophil/inflammatory cell infiltration and mucus, and increase beta-2 receptor expression.
  7. 7Canadian snow: Represents Candida albicans, a fungal opportunistic pathogen.
  8. 8Snow cone tongue: Inhaled corticosteroids can cause oropharyngeal candidiasis (thrush) and hoarseness; use a spacer and rinse the mouth after use.
  9. 9AA league: Arachidonic acid (AA), released from membrane phospholipids by phospholipase A2, is the precursor of both prostanoids (via COX) and leukotrienes (via LOX).
  10. 10Lacrosse Coach Lox: 5-lipoxygenase (5-LOX) converts arachidonic acid to leukotrienes.
  11. 11Lacrosse players: Leukotrienes LTB4, LTC4, LTD4 and LTE4 are important inflammatory mediators in asthma and allergy.
  12. 12B4 attracting first-responders: LTB4 is a chemoattractant for neutrophils and other inflammatory cells.
  13. 13First responders: Neutrophils, the first inflammatory cells recruited to injury, are chemotactically attracted by LTB4.
  14. 14Constricted lacrosse stick bronchi: The cysteinyl leukotrienes LTC4, LTD4 and LTE4 cause bronchoconstriction, increased vascular permeability and mucus production.
  15. 15Lacrosse goal CysLT1: Cysteinyl leukotriene receptor 1 (CysLT1) binds LTD4, the most potent bronchoconstrictor leukotriene.
  16. 16Monte the broadcaster: "-lukast" suffix: montelukast and zafirlukast are CysLT1 (LTD4) receptor antagonists.
  17. 17Blocked D4 shot: Leukotriene receptor antagonists are alternative or add-on therapy for mild persistent asthma and especially useful in aspirin-induced asthma and allergic rhinitis; they are used for prophylaxis, not acute attacks.⭐ Montelukast has a boxed warning for neuropsychiatric events.
  18. 18Dilated scarf: Blocking CysLT1 receptors reduces bronchoconstriction, causing bronchodilation and less inflammation.
  19. 19Open mouth: Leukotriene receptor antagonists are given orally.
  20. 20Godzilla falling on Coach Lox: Zileuton directly inhibits 5-lipoxygenase, blocking synthesis of all leukotrienes; an alternative therapy for mild persistent asthma.
  21. 21Liver spot: Zileuton can cause hepatotoxicity (monitor LFTs); it also inhibits CYP1A2 (raising theophylline levels).
  22. 22ASA umpire grabbing Coach Cox: Aspirin and NSAIDs inhibit COX, shunting arachidonic acid to the LOX pathway and increasing leukotriene production, which causes aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (asthma, nasal polyps, aspirin sensitivity).⭐ Treat AERD with LTRAs or zileuton and avoid NSAIDs.
  23. 23Salute formation: Salmeterol and formoterol are long-acting beta-2 agonists (LABA) added to ICS for moderate-to-severe persistent asthma.
  24. 24Long tapering flag: LABAs have a long duration of action (12 hours) due to lipophilic tails that anchor in the cell membrane.
  25. 25Inhaler: LABAs are used as daily controller inhalers, always combined with an inhaled corticosteroid because LABA monotherapy increases asthma-related death.⭐ Never use LABA alone in asthma.
  26. 26Higher moon face: Higher-dose inhaled corticosteroids are used in moderate-to-severe persistent asthma, with potential systemic effects at high doses.
  27. 27Xanthine energy drink: Methylxanthines (theophylline) are alternative add-on therapy for persistent asthma (and COPD) when other agents fail; rarely used due to toxicity.
  28. 28"flyin": Theophylline is the prototypical methylxanthine drug (aminophylline is its IV form).
  29. 29Caffeine: Methylxanthines are chemically related to caffeine and theobromine.
  30. 30"Don't phoster disinterest": Methylxanthines inhibit phosphodiesterase (preventing cAMP breakdown) and block adenosine receptors.
  31. 31"Camping": Increased cAMP in airway smooth muscle causes bronchodilation.
  32. 32Open mouth: Theophylline is administered orally (sustained-release); aminophylline can be given IV.
  33. 33Shaking kid: Methylxanthines cause CNS stimulation: nervousness, insomnia, tremor, and at toxic levels seizures.
  34. 34Holding up heart watch: Methylxanthines cause tachycardia and arrhythmias (cardiac stimulation); toxicity includes nausea, vomiting, seizures and ventricular arrhythmias.⭐ Narrow therapeutic index requires drug level monitoring.
  35. 35Chrome bumper hitting energy drinks: Theophylline is metabolized by hepatic CYP450 (CYP1A2), so inhibitors (ciprofloxacin, erythromycin, cimetidine, zileuton) raise levels and inducers (smoking, rifampin) lower them.
  36. 36Bee hive: Mast cell degranulation is key in the pathogenesis of allergic (atopic) asthma, releasing histamine, leukotrienes and prostaglandins.
  37. 37IgE gun shooting hive: Allergen binding to IgE bound to FcepsilonRI on mast cells causes cross-linking, degranulation and release of mediators (histamine, leukotrienes).
  38. 38Limousine: Omalizumab, a recombinant humanized anti-IgE monoclonal antibody ("-zumab"), is add-on therapy for moderate-to-severe persistent allergic asthma with elevated IgE.⭐ Risk of anaphylaxis.
  39. 39Grabbing end of IgE gun: Omalizumab binds the Fc portion of free IgE, preventing it from binding mast cell FcepsilonRI and thereby preventing mast cell sensitization and degranulation.
  40. 40Lynn's bee control: Cromolyn sodium is a mast cell stabilizer used for prophylaxis of asthma, allergic rhinitis and allergic conjunctivitis.
  41. 41Bee sedating smoke: Cromolyn sodium prevents mast cell degranulation and histamine release; it is ineffective for acute attacks and has little systemic toxicity.⭐ Prophylaxis only, not for acute asthma.
  42. 42Beta-2 tuba: Inhaled short-acting beta-2 agonists (albuterol), often repeated or nebulized, are first-line for acute severe asthma exacerbation.
  43. 43Moon face: Systemic corticosteroids (prednisone, methylprednisolone) are given in acute severe exacerbations to reduce airway inflammation and relapse.
  44. 44Ivy: Steroids are given intravenously (or orally) during an acute severe exacerbation; oral is equally effective if the patient can swallow.
  45. 45Cat-ipra-pillar: Nebulized ipratropium (a muscarinic antagonist) is added to SABA in severe exacerbations, blocking vagal-mediated bronchoconstriction and reducing hospitalization.
  46. 46Epic: Subcutaneous or intramuscular epinephrine (or terbutaline) is used in severe, refractory asthma exacerbation when inhaled therapy is not possible or effective.

2. Antihistamines

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: 1st generation - diphenhydramine, dimenhydrinate, chlorpheniramine, hydroxyzine, meclizine, promethazine, cyproheptadine; 2nd generation - loratadine, cetirizine, fexofenadine.
  • Mechanism: competitive (inverse agonist) blockade of H1 receptors (Gq: PLC, IP3, DAG, increased Ca2+); H2 receptors are Gs coupled and mediate gastric acid secretion.
  • Indications: allergic rhinitis, urticaria; 1st gen also motion sickness/vertigo, insomnia, pruritus, nausea and acute dystonia from antipsychotics.
  • Adverse effects of 1st gen: sedation, anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, urinary retention, constipation, blurred vision, delirium), anti-alpha-1 orthostasis, weight gain; avoid in elderly (Beers) and glaucoma/BPH.
  • 2nd gen: non-sedating, minimal CNS penetration; fexofenadine avoid with fruit juice; H2 blockers (cimetidine, famotidine) treat GERD, not allergy.
  1. 1Beehive: Histamine is stored in granules of mast cells and basophils and released on degranulation (e.g. IgE cross-linking in type I hypersensitivity).
  2. 2"Q" dandelion: H1 receptors are Gq-coupled (PLC activation, IP3/DAG, increased intracellular Ca2+), mediating allergic inflammatory effects.
  3. 3Honeypot with 2 "S" handles: H2 receptors are Gs-coupled (increased cAMP).
  4. 4Golden gastric honey: H2 receptor activation on gastric parietal cells stimulates H+/K+ ATPase and gastric acid secretion; H2 blockers (cimetidine, famotidine, ranitidine) reduce acid.
  5. 5Dripping nose sap: Histamine acting at H1 receptors increases nasal and bronchial mucus secretion (allergic rhinitis, rhinorrhea).
  6. 6Dripping vascular sap: H1 activation increases vascular permeability (via endothelial cell contraction), causing edema, urticaria/wheals and, with vasodilation, hypotension in anaphylaxis.
  7. 7Constricted lung branch: H1 activation contracts bronchial smooth muscle, causing bronchoconstriction.⭐ Antihistamines do not reliably treat asthma or anaphylaxis (epinephrine does).
  8. 8Brain tree: Histamine is a CNS neurotransmitter (tuberomammillary nucleus) promoting wakefulness; H1 blockade that crosses the blood-brain barrier causes sedation.
  9. 9Bee swatter: H1 receptor blockers (antihistamines) are competitive antagonists/inverse agonists used for allergic conditions.
  10. 10Dragonfly fairy: Diphenhydramine and dimenhydrinate are first-generation H1 blockers.
  11. 11Color fairy: Chlorpheniramine (also brompheniramine) is a first-generation H1 blocker.
  12. 12Fairy cuiSINE: Hydroxyzine, meclizine and promethazine are first-generation H1 blockers; hydroxyzine is used for pruritus and anxiety, promethazine is an antiemetic (also D2 blocker), meclizine treats vertigo.
  13. 13Fairy dust and dander: H1 blockers treat type I hypersensitivity: allergic rhinitis, urticaria, allergic conjunctivitis; second-generation agents are preferred as first-line.
  14. 14Seasick fairy sailors: First-generation H1 blockers (dimenhydrinate, meclizine, diphenhydramine, promethazine) treat motion sickness, vertigo and nausea because they are lipophilic, cross the BBB and block H1/muscarinic receptors in the vestibular system and brainstem.
  15. 15Sleeping under brain tree: First-generation H1 blockers cause sedation due to CNS H1 blockade; diphenhydramine and doxylamine are used as OTC sleep aids.⭐ Sedation worsens with alcohol and other CNS depressants.
  16. 16Antimuscarinic tea party: First-generation H1 blockers block muscarinic receptors causing dry mouth, mydriasis/blurred vision, urinary retention, constipation, tachycardia and delirium; use cautiously in glaucoma and BPH.⭐ Atropine-like: "blind as a bat, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter."
  17. 17Falling "extra parking" cone: Diphenhydramine treats acute dystonia (extrapyramidal side effect of antipsychotics) because its anticholinergic effect restores the dopamine-acetylcholine balance (benztropine is the other option).
  18. 18Cut smiley face cake: Some first-generation H1 blockers (especially cyproheptadine) block serotonin (5-HT2) receptors in the CNS.
  19. 19Stuffed fairy: Antiserotonergic effects increase appetite and cause weight gain; cyproheptadine is used as an appetite stimulant and for serotonin syndrome and carcinoid symptoms.
  20. 20Extinguished single alpha candle: First-generation H1 blockers block alpha-1 adrenergic receptors, causing vasodilation.
  21. 21Fainted fairy: Alpha-1 blockade produces dizziness, orthostatic hypotension and syncope.
  22. 22Delirious elderly man: First-generation H1 blockers cause confusion, delirium and cognitive impairment in the elderly (central antihistamine and anticholinergic effects); they are on the Beers list.⭐ Avoid diphenhydramine in the elderly.
  23. 23Fox, satyr, and rat: Second-generation H1 blockers: loratadine, desloratadine, fexofenadine, cetirizine (levocetirizine); "-adine"/"-izine" are common name cues.
  24. 24Fox, satyr, and rat stand outside of brain tree: Second-generation H1 blockers are less lipophilic and are substrates of the P-glycoprotein efflux pump at the BBB, so little CNS penetration: non-sedating, with minimal anticholinergic, antiserotonergic and anti-alpha effects.⭐ Cetirizine is the most sedating of the second-generation agents.

3. Opioids, Naloxone, Naltrexone

Exam snapshot
  • Opioid receptors (mu, kappa, delta) are Gi/o-coupled: open K+ channels, close presynaptic Ca2+ channels, and reduce neurotransmitter release; mu mediates analgesia, sedation, respiratory depression, miosis, constipation.
  • Agents: morphine, fentanyl, tramadol (weak mu agonist plus SNRI action, lowers seizure threshold), loperamide and diphenoxylate (antidiarrheals), codeine and dextromethorphan (antitussives; dextromethorphan is an NMDA antagonist).
  • Toxicity: respiratory depression, pinpoint pupils, constipation, biliary colic, sedation; tolerance develops to most effects except miosis and constipation; chronic use can cause hyperalgesia and withdrawal with rhinorrhea, yawning, diarrhea, and anxiety.
  • Opioid use disorder treatment: methadone (long-acting full agonist) and buprenorphine (long-acting partial mu agonist); partial agonists can precipitate withdrawal; neonatal abstinence syndrome in exposed infants.
  • Antagonists: naloxone reverses acute overdose (short-acting, can precipitate withdrawal); naltrexone maintains abstinence in opioid and alcohol use disorder and is used for weight loss with bupropion.
  1. 1Utopia: Opiates are drugs derived from or acting like opium (poppy) alkaloids, acting on endogenous opioid receptors (mu, kappa, delta) for analgesia and related effects.
  2. 2Mu massage: The mu-opioid receptor mediates most clinical and adverse effects: analgesia, euphoria, sedation, respiratory depression, miosis, and constipation.⭐ Kappa: spinal analgesia, dysphoria, miosis. Delta: analgesia, antidepressant-like effects.check original
  3. 3Open banana barrels: Opioid receptors are Gi/o-coupled; they open K+ channels, causing efflux and hyperpolarization of the postsynaptic neuron.
  4. 4Closed Calci-Yum ice cream cooler: Opioid receptors close presynaptic voltage-gated Ca2+ channels and decrease cAMP.
  5. 5Disconnected presynaptic wire: Closing presynaptic voltage-gated Ca2+ channels prevents neurotransmitter release (glutamate, acetylcholine, norepinephrine, serotonin, substance P), which blunts pain transmission.
  6. 6Fantasy: Fentanyl is a potent, short-acting synthetic opioid analgesic (about 100x morphine), used IV, epidural, and transdermally for severe pain and anesthesia.
  7. 7More fun: Morphine is the prototype opioid analgesic used for severe pain, including acute MI pain and acute pulmonary edema (reduces preload and anxiety).
  8. 8Distant tram: Tramadol is a weak mu-opioid receptor agonist used to manage chronic pain.
  9. 9North-South: Tramadol also inhibits reuptake of norepinephrine and serotonin.⭐ Tramadol lowers the seizure threshold and can cause serotonin syndrome, especially with SSRIs, SNRIs, or MAOIs.
  10. 10Colon massage table: Opioid receptors are located in the GI tract; activation delays stool transit and increases fluid absorption, causing constipation.check original
  11. 11Removed muddy slippers: Opioids such as loperamide and diphenoxylate are used as antidiarrheals.
  12. 12Lop-eared rabbit: Loperamide is an opioid antidiarrheal that acts on gut mu-receptors and has minimal CNS effects because it does not cross the blood-brain barrier well (P-glycoprotein efflux).⭐ Loperamide is contraindicated in invasive or bloody diarrhea and C. difficile colitis. Very high doses can cause cardiac toxicity (QT prolongation).
  13. 13Loping back and forth: Loperamide increases colonic phasic segmentation, which slows propulsion and increases stool transit time.
  14. 14Dolphins: Diphenoxylate is an opioid antidiarrheal, combined with atropine (Lomotil) to discourage abuse.
  15. 15Barcode: Codeine is an opioid antitussive (cough suppressant) and a weak analgesic.⭐ Codeine is a prodrug activated to morphine by CYP2D6; ultrarapid metabolizers are at risk of toxicity (avoid in children and breastfeeding mothers).
  16. 16Orphan: Dextromethorphan is a nonaddictive antitussive that is a synthetic codeine analog.
  17. 17Tethered nomadic camel: Dextromethorphan antagonizes NMDA receptors; at high doses it can cause dissociative effects and hallucinations.⭐ Dextromethorphan with MAOIs or SSRIs can cause serotonin syndrome.
  18. 18Cerebral towel: Opiates cause CNS depression, including sedation, and can cause euphoria.
  19. 19Deflated lung vest: Opiates can cause respiratory depression by decreasing the brainstem response to CO2. This is the usual cause of death in overdose.
  20. 20Constricted hood: Opiates cause miosis (pinpoint pupils) via stimulation of the Edinger-Westphal nucleus.⭐ Pinpoint pupils plus respiratory depression and coma is the classic opioid overdose triad. Meperidine is an exception that can cause mydriasis in overdose.
  21. 21Plunger: Opiates cause constipation, because of decreased GI motility and secretions.
  22. 22Biliary tree: Opiates can cause biliary colic by contracting biliary smooth muscle and the sphincter of Oddi.
  23. 23All are welcome: Patients can develop tolerance to opiates, including to analgesia, euphoria, sedation, and respiratory depression.
  24. 24Out of order: Tolerance does not develop to miosis or constipation, so these effects persist with chronic use.
  25. 25Causing pain: Opioid-induced hyperalgesia (increased pain sensitivity) can occur with chronic opioid use.
  26. 26Anxious, hot, and moist: Opioid withdrawal causes rhinorrhea, lacrimation, yawning, hyperventilation, hyperthermia, muscle aches, vomiting, diarrhea, and anxiety. It is very unpleasant but rarely life-threatening.⭐ Treat with methadone or buprenorphine; clonidine can reduce autonomic symptoms.
  27. 27DONE timer: Methadone is a long-acting opioid (full mu agonist) used to attenuate withdrawal symptoms and for maintenance therapy of opioid use disorder; it can also treat chronic pain.⭐ Methadone can prolong the QT interval and is also an NMDA antagonist.
  28. 28Long tapering flag: Methadone and buprenorphine have long half-lives, which makes them useful in opioid detoxification and maintenance with less euphoria and fewer withdrawal swings.
  29. 29Blueprint: Buprenorphine is a long-acting partial mu-opioid agonist used to attenuate withdrawal symptoms and for maintenance treatment of opioid use disorder.⭐ Often given sublingually combined with naloxone to deter IV misuse. Has a ceiling effect on respiratory depression, so it is safer in overdose than full agonists.
  30. 30Irritable, moist, tachypneic baby: Neonatal abstinence syndrome occurs in infants of opioid-dependent mothers: diarrhea, sweating, sneezing, crying, tachypnea, and irritability.
  31. 31Partial massage: Partial mu-opioid agonists (buprenorphine, and by the scene nalbuphine and butorphanol) activate the receptor with lower intrinsic activity than full agonists.⭐ Nalbuphine and butorphanol are kappa agonists with mu partial agonist or antagonist activity.check original
  32. 32Bluefin: The 'bu-phin' stem marks the partial opioid agonists, such as buprenorphine, nalbuphine, and butorphanol.
  33. 33Falling into the withdraw spa: Partial mu-opioid agonists can precipitate withdrawal in patients dependent on full agonists, because they displace the full agonist while providing less receptor activation.
  34. 34No lax zone: Naloxone is a mu-opioid receptor antagonist (competitive) used to reverse acute opioid toxicity. It can precipitate withdrawal in dependent patients.⭐ Naloxone has a short half-life (1-2 hours) so repeat dosing or an infusion may be needed after long-acting opioids like methadone.
  35. 35No tricks zone: Naltrexone is a long-acting opioid receptor antagonist that helps maintain abstinence in patients with opioid (heroin) use disorder, given orally or as a monthly injection.⭐ Patients must be opioid-free before starting or it precipitates withdrawal. Monitor liver function.
  36. 36Tempting alcohol: Naltrexone (mu-opioid antagonist) helps reduce cravings for alcohol and nicotine.⭐ Naltrexone is first-line for alcohol use disorder, in contrast to disulfiram or acamprosate.
  37. 37Getting fit: Naltrexone can help with weight loss, as when combined with bupropion.

4. Broad-Spectrum Antiepileptics

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: valproate, topiramate, lamotrigine, levetiracetam, brivaracetam.
  • Mechanism: valproate - Na+ and T-type Ca2+ blockade, raises GABA; topiramate - Na+ block, GABA-A potentiation, AMPA block; lamotrigine - Na+ block; levetiracetam/brivaracetam - SV2A.
  • Indications: focal and generalized seizures; valproate JME and bipolar; topiramate migraine prophylaxis; lamotrigine bipolar maintenance and Lennox-Gastaut.
  • Adverse: valproate hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, tremor, weight gain, neural tube defects; topiramate kidney stones, glaucoma, cognitive slowing, weight loss; lamotrigine SJS; levetiracetam irritability/depression.
  • Buzzwords: valproate = hyperammonemia and spina bifida; topiramate = 'Dopamax', stones, glaucoma; lamotrigine rash (slow titration); levetiracetam binds SV2A.
  1. 1One-arm shaking: Focal seizures originate in one hemisphere and are either aware (previously 'simple') or impaired awareness ('complex'); a patient who is aware but cannot talk or respond has a focal aware seizure.
  2. 2Shaking structure: Generalized seizures involve both hemispheres: motor (tonic-clonic: stiffening then jerking) or non-motor (absence: staring, brief unresponsiveness, automatisms).
  3. 3Rainbow goggles: Broad-spectrum antiepileptics (valproate, topiramate, lamotrigine, levetiracetam) treat both focal and generalized seizures.
  4. 4Shaking Man FestiVAL: Valproate (valproic acid) is a broad-spectrum antiepileptic used for focal and generalized seizures and bipolar disorder; the drug of choice for juvenile myoclonic epilepsy (except in women of childbearing potential).
  5. 5Sold Out peanuts and Calci-Yum ice cream: Valproate blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels (increases inactivation) and T-type Ca2+ channels, preventing repetitive neuronal firing.
  6. 6CAB-A: Valproate increases GABA levels in the CNS by inhibiting GABA transaminase, stimulating glutamic acid decarboxylase and enhancing GABA release.
  7. 7Nauseated reveller: Valproate commonly causes GI distress (nausea, vomiting); taking it with food helps.
  8. 8Heavy box: Valproate increases appetite and causes weight gain.
  9. 9Tremoring antenna: Valproate can cause tremor (dose-related).
  10. 10Liver fanny pack + ammonia cleaner: Valproate can cause hyperammonemia (even with normal LFTs) and rare fatal hepatotoxicity (especially in young children and with metabolic disease); baseline and periodic LFTs, although monitoring does not reliably predict liver failure.⭐ Valproate: hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, teratogenicity (neural tube defects), hyperammonemia.
  11. 11Squeezed pancreas sponge: Valproate can cause pancreatitis (rare, idiosyncratic, not dose- or duration-dependent).
  12. 12Tubular tarantula: Valproate is highly teratogenic: neural tube defects (spina bifida), orofacial clefts, cardiac, urogenital and limb defects, and lower IQ; avoid in pregnancy; give folic acid.
  13. 13Toupee: Topiramate is a broad-spectrum antiepileptic used for focal and generalized seizures, Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, and migraine prophylaxis (also used with phentermine for weight loss; for absence seizures it is not first-line).
  14. 14Locked peanut boxes: Topiramate blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels, stabilizing neuronal membranes; it also blocks AMPA/kainate (glutamate) receptors.
  15. 15CAB-A pedicab passenger: Topiramate potentiates GABA-A activity by binding a non-benzodiazepine site.⭐ Topiramate: weak carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, causing metabolic acidosis and kidney stones.
  16. 16Sleeping driver: Topiramate can cause somnolence, fatigue, confusion, cognitive slowing and word-finding difficulty ('Dopamax').
  17. 17Skinny driver: Topiramate causes weight loss and anorexia; it is combined with phentermine for obesity (Qsymia).
  18. 18Kidney-shaped wheels: Topiramate can cause nephrolithiasis (calcium phosphate stones, due to carbonic anhydrase inhibition and urinary alkalinization); advise hydration.
  19. 19Bulging eye balloon: Topiramate can cause acute angle-closure glaucoma (usually in the first month); stop immediately if visual blurring or eye pain occurs.
  20. 20Llama: Lamotrigine is a broad-spectrum antiepileptic used for focal and generalized seizures, Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, bipolar disorder (maintenance); absence seizures (less effective than valproate or ethosuximide).
  21. 21Locked peanut box: Lamotrigine blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels, also reducing glutamate release.
  22. 22Cross-eyed: Lamotrigine can cause diplopia, blurred vision, dizziness, and ataxia.
  23. 23Sloughed off red mask: Lamotrigine can cause Stevens-Johnson syndrome/TEN (higher risk with rapid titration, valproate co-administration, and in children) as well as benign rash.⭐ Valproate doubles lamotrigine levels; titrate slowly and reduce dose.
  24. 24Elevator: Levetiracetam is a broad-spectrum antiepileptic for focal, myoclonic and generalized tonic-clonic seizures; it binds synaptic vesicle glycoprotein 2A (SV2A), modulating neurotransmitter release.⭐ Levetiracetam has minimal drug interactions (renally cleared, no CYP induction).
  25. 25Brave: Brivaracetam is an SV2A ligand used for focal-onset seizures (a more limited indication than levetiracetam).
  26. 26Dizzy, nauseated rider: Levetiracetam and brivaracetam can cause dizziness, somnolence and nausea.
  27. 27Falling brain hat: Levetiracetam (and less so brivaracetam) can cause neuropsychiatric effects: irritability, depression, hostility, aggression, agitation; higher risk with psychiatric history.
  28. 28P450 shoulder plates: Brivaracetam serum concentration is decreased with CYP450 inducers (phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital); it is also partly metabolized by CYP2C19.

5. Carbamazepine, Oxcarbazepine, Phenytoin, Tiagabine, Vigabatrin

Exam snapshot
  • Drugs: carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenytoin (fosphenytoin IV), tiagabine, vigabatrin; benzodiazepines and phenobarbital for status epilepticus.
  • Mechanism: carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine and phenytoin prolong Na+ channel inactivation; tiagabine blocks GABA reuptake (GAT-1); vigabatrin irreversibly inhibits GABA transaminase (raises GABA).
  • Uses: focal and generalized tonic-clonic seizures; carbamazepine is first line for trigeminal neuralgia; status epilepticus: IV benzodiazepine first, phenytoin for maintenance, phenobarbital if refractory.
  • Adverse effects: carbamazepine - agranulocytosis, SIADH, DRESS, SJS/TEN (screen HLA-B*1502), teratogenic, CYP450 inducer (lowers hormonal contraceptive levels); phenytoin - gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism, folate deficiency, low bone density, drug-induced lupus, cleft palate; both - ataxia and diplopia.
  • Buzzwords: oxcarbazepine = carbamazepine with fewer side effects and interactions; fetal hydantoin syndrome; vigabatrin can cause permanent visual field loss.
  1. 1Seize the Night: Narrow-spectrum antiepileptics include carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital.

9. Inhaled Anesthetics & Dantrolene

Exam snapshot

  5. 5Moving freely in ball pit: Blood solubility determines how readily the agent leaves blood for the brain: agents of low blood solubility (N2O, desflurane, sevoflurane) move freely into the brain, while highly soluble agents (halothane) are held up in blood.check original

  7. 7Passed out earlier: Less soluble agents (N2O, desflurane) have faster onset of action because alveolar and arterial partial pressures equilibrate quickly.

  18. 18Deflating lung balloons: Inhaled anesthetics cause respiratory depression with decreased minute ventilation and hypercapnia (and decreased response to hypoxia/CO2).
  19. 19Red brain wig: Fluorinated volatile anesthetics increase cerebral blood flow (cerebral vasodilation, lower cerebrovascular resistance) and ICP, though they reduce metabolic rate.

10. Parkinsonism Drugs

Exam snapshot
